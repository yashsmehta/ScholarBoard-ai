# Adam Green

*Cognitive neuroscience of creativity and reasoning* — Georgetown University, Department of Psychology, Lab for Relational Cognition.

## Background

Adam E. Green is a cognitive neuroscientist who focuses on the neural architecture of human creative intelligence and relational reasoning. Green's research utilizes functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and electrical neuromodulation to investigate how the frontopolar cortex supports the integration of semantically distant concepts during analogical mapping. They have worked on the quantification of creativity through the study of 'semantic distance' and the characterization of creativity as a dynamic neural state rather than a fixed trait. Their current work examines the neuroscience of learning in STEM education and compares human versus artificial intelligence in creative visual tasks.

## Papers

### 2026 — The Creative Link Between Words and Ideas is Weakening in the AI Era
*PsyArXiv (Preprint) · 0 citations*
Authors: Kibum Moon, Kostadin Kushlev, Andrew Bank, Adam Green

In a series of large-scale natural experiments utilizing 372,793 personal statements, we tested the 'disjunctive homogenization' hypothesis following the pervasive adoption of Large Language Models (LLMs). Our findings demonstrate a bifurcation in semantic diversity: post-LLM release, writing exhibits increased word-level variety alongside a significant decrease in conceptual diversity at the whole-document and sentence levels. We propose that LLMs drive a shift toward linguistic superficiality, weakening the intrinsic association between lexical diversity and conceptual innovation found in human-exclusive writing.

### 2025 — Brain networks supporting scientific creative thinking
*Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts · 30 citations*
Authors: Roger E. Beaty, Robert A. Cortes, Harrison M. Merseal, Mariale M. Hardiman, Adam E. Green

This study employs connectome-based predictive modeling (CPM) to identify functional neural signatures associated with divergent ideation in scientific problem-solving. Functional connectivity analysis of a high-resolution fMRI dataset revealed that individual variability in scientific creativity is robustly predicted by the task-dependent integration of the default mode network (DMN) and the frontoparietal control network (FPCN). These results elucidate the role of large-scale network coupling in supporting the generative and evaluative phases of creative thinking within STEM-specific domains.

### 2025 — Neurocognitive correlates of spatial thinking mediate the relation between socioeconomic status and STEM achievement
*Scientific Reports · 0 citations*
Authors: Jazelle Pilato, Robert Cortes, Emily Grossnickle Peterson, David Uttal, Robert Kolvoord, Adam Green

We investigated the neurocognitive mechanisms mediating the relationship between socioeconomic status (SES) and STEM achievement in adolescents. Utilizing functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) during a mental transformation task, we identified that SES-related disparities in academic outcomes are significantly mediated by variations in premotor cortex activation and spatial reasoning efficiency. These findings suggest that the socioeconomic gradient in STEM success is partially underpinned by differences in the neural recruitment of frontoparietal circuits dedicated to spatial mental modeling.

### 2025 — Creativity Anxiety: Evidence for Anxiety That Is Specific to Creative Thinking, From STEM to the Arts
*Journal of Experimental Psychology: General · 119 citations*
Authors: Richard J. Daker, Robert A. Cortes, Ian M. Lyons, Adam E. Green

This research establishes 'creativity anxiety' as a distinct and generalizable psychometric construct separate from broader anxiety dimensions. Utilizing the Creativity Anxiety Scale (CAS), we demonstrate that anxiety specific to creative ideation uniquely predicts performance decrements and behavioral avoidance in innovative tasks within both artistic and STEM contexts. These effects persist after controlling for general trait anxiety, math anxiety, and intelligence, highlighting creativity-specific apprehension as a primary cognitive barrier to the actualization of creative potential.

### 2024 — The Scientific Creative Thinking Test (SCTT): Reliability, Validity, and Automated Scoring
*PsyArXiv (Preprint) · 7 citations*
Authors: Roger E. Beaty, Robert A. Cortes, Simone A. Luchini, John D. Patterson, Boris Forthmann, Brendan S. Baker, Baptiste Barbot, Mariale M. Hardiman, Adam Green

We introduce the Scientific Creative Thinking Test (SCTT), a novel psychometric battery designed to measure divergent ideation in STEM through subtests for hypothesis generation and experimental design. The study validates the SCTT's reliability and internal structure while demonstrating measurement invariance across gender and race. Furthermore, we developed an automated scoring pipeline using a large language model (Llama 2) fine-tuned on human expert ratings, providing a computationally efficient and objective methodology for assessing STEM-specific creative potential at scale.
