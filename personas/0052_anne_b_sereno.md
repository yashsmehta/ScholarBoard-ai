# Anne B Sereno

*attention, memory, and eye movements* — Purdue University, Department of Psychological Sciences and Weldon School of Biomedical Engineering, Sereno Lab.

## Background

Anne B. Sereno is a Professor of Psychological Sciences and Biomedical Engineering at Purdue University, where Sereno investigates the physiological mechanisms of visual attention, short-term memory, and eye movements. Their research combines behavioral psychophysics, extracellular recordings, and computational modeling to examine how cortical pathways process sensorimotor and cognitive information. A portion of Sereno’s work involves translational neuroscience, specifically the development of eye-tracking and sensorimotor technologies as biomarkers for neurological disorders. These tools are designed to assist in the diagnosis and monitoring of conditions such as traumatic brain injury, Parkinson’s disease, and autism spectrum disorder.

## Papers

### 2024 — Understanding Cortical Streams from a Computational Perspective
*Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience · 3 citations*
Authors: Zhixian Han, Anne B. Sereno

This review re-evaluates the classical dual-stream hypothesis of visual processing, which distinguishes between ventral 'what' and dorsal 'where' pathways, through the framework of computational modeling. Utilizing feed-forward convolutional neural networks (CNNs), the authors demonstrate that while shape and spatial information may be present in both streams, they are independently constructed and differently retained according to the specific task goals of each pathway. The research highlights the computational advantages of segregation, showing that two-pathway architectures achieve higher testing accuracy and greater learning efficiency than single-pathway networks, particularly as scene complexity and the number of visual objects increase. The study concludes that specialized pathways are necessary for optimal object recognition and localization and discusses how these segregated sub-pathways address the binding problem in complex visual scenes.

### 2024 — Exploring neural architectures for simultaneously recognizing multiple visual attributes
*Scientific Reports · 1 citations*
Authors: Zhixian Han, Anne B. Sereno

In this study, the authors investigate whether the computational advantage of segregated neural architectures extends to the simultaneous recognition of various visual attributes such as luminance, orientation, identity, and location. By training multi-branch convolutional neural networks on diverse combinations of these attributes, the simulations reveal that segregated pathways consistently outperform single-pathway models across all tested conditions. This performance gap increases as the number of classes within an attribute grows or as the task requires finer discrimination. The findings suggest that specialization into specialized sub-streams is a fundamental strategy for maximizing learning efficiency in the primate visual system, providing a computational justification for the functional modularity observed in higher visual processing areas.

### 2024 — A spatial map: a propitious choice for constraining the binding problem
*Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience · 5 citations*
Authors: Zhixian Han, Anne B. Sereno

This computational study addresses the 'binding problem'—the mechanism by which the brain correctly associates multiple features of an object—by evaluating the efficacy of different attribute-based maps within two-pathway neural networks. The authors compared networks constrained by identity, luminance, orientation, and location maps during multi-attribute recognition tasks. Results demonstrate that a location-based spatial map consistently yields superior accuracy in correctly binding features to their respective objects compared to other attribute maps, even when the pathways are not explicitly trained for spatial recognition. These findings align with neurophysiological evidence of pervasive retinotopic organization in the visual cortex, suggesting that spatial maps provide a robust computational constraint for maintaining object integrity in complex visual environments.

### 2024 — A machine-learning method isolating changes in wrist kinematics that identify age-related changes in arm movement
*Scientific Reports · 2 citations*
Authors: Aditya Shanghavi, Daniel Larranaga, Rhutuja Patil, Elizabeth M. Frazier, Satyajit Ambike, Bradley S. Duerstock, Anne B. Sereno

The research employs wearable inertial measurement units (IMUs) and machine learning classifiers to isolate and identify age-related changes in motor function and wrist kinematics. Data from younger and older adults performing postural and pronation-supination tasks were analyzed using frequency decomposition and Support Vector Machines (SVM). Kinematic analysis identified significant differences in physiological tremor amplitudes within the 9–13 Hz range, which reliably distinguished older from younger participants. Furthermore, older adults exhibited a 71 ms delay in movement initiation during active tasks. The SVM classifier achieved an AUROC of 0.86 and 89% accuracy in identifying age groups based on simple kinematic variables, demonstrating that low-cost, non-invasive sensors can provide sensitive objective biomarkers for age-related motor slowing and physiological tremors.

### 2024 — Replicability in texture segmentation: Beck's (1966) classic experiments revisited
*Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics*
Authors: Maria Kon, Anne B. Sereno

This study provides a large-scale psychophysical replication of Jacob Beck's (1966) foundational experiments on texture segmentation, which established that local orientation differences are more salient cues for segregation than complex shape differences (e.g., T vs. L). The authors utilized a large participant pool to confirm the robust nature of Beck's original findings and extended the research by applying a biologically plausible computational model of visual grouping and selection. The model successfully explains the experimental results through the mechanisms of top-down selection and bottom-up feature salience, emphasizing the role of early visual cortical selectivities in spontaneous scene segmentation. The work reinforces the validity of orientation-based accounts of pre-attentive texture processing while providing a modern computational framework for understanding these classic perceptual phenomena.
