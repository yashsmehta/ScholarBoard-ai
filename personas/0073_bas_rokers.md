# Bas Rokers

*visual perception, motion, and depth* — New York University Abu Dhabi, Department of Psychology; Center for Neural Science, Rokers Vision Laboratory.

## Background

Bas Rokers is a Professor of Psychology and the Director of the Center for Brain and Health at New York University Abu Dhabi. Rokers investigates the neural mechanisms of three-dimensional visual perception, specifically focusing on how the brain integrates motion and depth cues to construct a representation of the environment. Utilizing a combination of behavioral psychophysics, neuroimaging (fMRI and tractography), and computational modeling, they explore the transformation of sensory signals into perceptual states. Their research also addresses practical applications in health and technology, including the treatment of amblyopia and the optimization of virtual reality systems to mitigate motion sickness and improve immersive experiences.

## Papers

### 2025 — The ASPIRE Research Institute Brain Health Dataset: Building a Foundation for Brain Health Research in the United Arab Emirates
*Scientific Data*
Authors: Abdalla Z. Mohamed, Ameen Qadi, Amna Dogar, Amal Salah, Aisha Hamkari, Dana Alkalali, Gul Begum, Haidee J. Paterson, Kartik K. Sreenivasan, Milos Ljubisavljevic, Osama Abdullah, Omnia Hassanin, Puti Wen, Rawand Benour, Soumen Mohanty, Yvonne Valles, Bas Rokers

This study details the establishment of the first large-scale normative neuroimaging repository tailored to the United Arab Emirates (UAE) population, aimed at addressing the scarcity of controlled brain health data in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA). The protocol implements a comprehensive multimodal imaging battery, including high-resolution T1-weighted structural MRI, diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) for microstructural assessment, resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) for connectivity analysis, and arterial spin labeling (ASL) for cerebral blood flow measurement. Psychophysical evaluations emphasize visual function through metrics such as ETDRS visual acuity, Pelli-Robson contrast sensitivity, and Randot stereoacuity, alongside VR-based perimetry. This initial release provides open-access data for a cohort of 41 participants, accompanied by standardized preprocessing pipelines to facilitate regional and global comparative neuroscience research.

### 2025 — Functional localization of visual motion area FST in humans
*Imaging Neuroscience · 3 citations*
Authors: Puti Wen, Rania Ezzo, Lowell W. Thompson, Ari Rosenberg, Michael S. Landy, Bas Rokers

The human homolog of the macaque fundus of the superior temporal sulcus (FST) has been difficult to delineate due to its proximity to the hMT+ complex. Here, researchers evaluated functional localizers to distinguish putative FST (pFST) from hMT and MST using 2D and 3D motion stimuli alongside population receptive field (pRF) mapping. Results revealed a distinct area localized anterior and inferior to hMT/MST that exhibited selective activation for coherent 3D motion (stereomotion) over 2D retinal motion. This functional localization was further validated through measures of motion opponency and T1-weighted signal intensity (myelination), which demonstrated that pFST possesses unique structural and functional properties consistent with its specialized role in processing complex motion signals such as structure-from-motion and looming.

### 2025 — Single-trial fMRI decoding of 3D motion with stereoscopic and perspective cues
*The Journal of Neuroscience · 2 citations*
Authors: Puti Wen, Lowell W. Thompson, Ari Rosenberg, Michael S. Landy, Bas Rokers

This investigation utilized multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) to decode 3D motion direction (toward vs. away) from single-trial BOLD responses in the human visual cortex, specifically focusing on the hMT+ complex sub-regions (MT, MST, and FST). By manipulating stereoscopic and perspective cues, the study found that MT exhibits superior decoding accuracy for perspective-driven motion, whereas FST demonstrates a specialization for stereoscopic depth cues. Quantitative analysis revealed that decoding in V1 and MST was largely driven by retinotopic biases, while MT and FST representations were more robust to such confounds. Furthermore, individual variance in stereoscopic proficiency correlated specifically with FST decoding performance, and control experiments established that FST activity is uniquely influenced by the perceptual experience of 3D motion rather than just low-level binocular disparity.

### 2025 — Integrating Visual Input from Both Eyes: Binocular Retinotopic Organization based on Monocular Input
*Journal of Vision*
Authors: Abdalla Z. Mohamed, Omnia Hassanin, Rania Ezzo, Alessio Fracasso, Jonathan A. Winawer, Bas Rokers

To investigate the neural mechanisms of binocular integration, this study compared population receptive field (pRF) properties under monocular and binocular stimulation across the visual hierarchy (V1–hV4 and TO1). While binocular stimulation elicited greater BOLD response amplitudes, the summation was subadditive, consistent with neural normalization models. Notably, binocular input resulted in a significant increase in pRF size specifically in V1, a finding that was not observed in higher extrastriate areas. This expansion is mathematically consistent with models of monocular pRF center mismatches within a single voxel, suggesting that binocular representations in early visual cortex are formed by the integration of slightly offset monocular inputs rather than a simple linear combination of signals.

### 2024 — Quantitative evaluation of lesion load in multiple sclerosis using multimodal MR
*Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders*
Authors: Puti Wen, Osama Abdullah, Lev Brylev, Victor Bryukhov, Dana Alkalali, Mariya Matrosova, Bas Rokers

This research addresses the clinico-radiological paradox in Multiple Sclerosis (MS) by proposing a tract-based approach to lesion analysis that integrates spatial localization with microstructural composition. Using multimodal 3T MRI—including FLAIR, T1 mapping, magnetization transfer ratio (MTR), and diffusion tensor imaging (DTI)—the study quantified lesion burden within specific white matter tracts instead of broad anatomical regions. Findings indicate that lesion metrics filtered through functionally critical tracts (e.g., occipitoparietal or corticospinal) are significantly better predictors of Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) scores and cognitive impairment than total lesion volume. Specifically, tract-based T1 values and fractional anisotropy (FA) provided high accuracy in stratifying relapsing-remitting patients into stable versus progressive risk categories (AUC = 0.90), offering a more precise biomarker for individualized disease monitoring.
