# Bradford Z. Mahon

*cognitive neuroscience of object representation* — Carnegie Mellon University, Department of Psychology and the Neuroscience Institute, Concepts, Actions, and Objects (CAOs) Lab.

## Background

Bradford Mahon is a cognitive neuroscientist who investigates how the human brain organizes and represents object concepts and their associated motor and linguistic information. Utilizing functional MRI and behavioral studies, Mahon examines how visual input is transformed into conceptual knowledge to guide object-directed actions and naming. Their research involves studying patients with focal brain injuries from stroke or tumors, including translational work that utilizes awake brain mapping during neurosurgery to map language and motor functions. This approach facilitates causal tests of the neurocognitive organization of the ventral and dorsal visual pathways and has led to the development of surgical decision-support tools for preserving cognitive functions.

## Papers

### 2026 — Causal parametric language mapping with electrical stimulation during awake neurosurgery
*Science Advances*
Authors: J. Raouf Belkhir, W. Tecumseh Fitch, Frank E. Garcea, Benjamin L. Chernoff, Max H. Sims, Eduardo Navarrete, Sam Haber, David A. Paul, Susan O. Smith, Webster H. Pilcher, Bradford Z. Mahon

This study employs intraoperative direct electrical stimulation (DES) to delineate language-sensitive cortical architecture during awake craniotomies. By systematically manipulating the physical parameters of cortical perturbation—specifically timing, duration, and onset latencies relative to linguistic task phases (e.g., picture naming and word reading)—the researchers demonstrate that these variables significantly modulate behavioral outcomes. Meta-analysis of granular performance data reveals that subtle variations in the mapping protocol, previously categorized as non-diagnostic noise, provide statistically meaningful signals regarding the underlying functional topography. This parametric framework facilitates the development of high-fidelity predictive models of individual brain organization, potentially optimizing the trade-off between maximal tumor cytoreduction and the preservation of critical language networks.

### 2025 — Left-hemisphere glioma drives systematic patterns of contralesional functional connectivity
*Brain Communications*
Authors: Emma Strawderman, Frank E. Garcea, Madalina E. Tivarus, Steven P. Meyers, Adnan A. Hirad, William M. Burns, Kevin A. Walter, Tyler Schmidt, Webster H. Pilcher, Bradford Z. Mahon

This research utilizes machine learning to evaluate the systemic impact of unilateral left-hemisphere gliomas on the contralesional functional connectome. Analyzing resting-state functional MRI data from a cohort of 48 glioma patients and 107 neurotypical controls, the study demonstrates that focal tumors induce consistent, predictable alterations in right-hemisphere (RH) functional connectivity patterns. Support vector machine (SVM) classifiers successfully distinguished patients from controls (89% accuracy) and identified clinically relevant molecular subtypes, such as isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutation status (78% accuracy). Crucially, the patterns of RH connectivity were found to be predictive of the spatial coordinates of left-hemisphere lesions, particularly those involving the parietal lobe. These findings provide empirical support for large-scale functional reorganization and highlight the potential for using contralesional connectivity as a biomarker for disease progression and neural plasticity.

### 2025 — Object-directed action representations are componentially built in parietal cortex
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS) · 2 citations*
Authors: Leyla Roksan Caglar, Jon Walbrin, Emefa Akwayena, Jorge Almeida, Bradford Z. Mahon

The human inferior parietal lobule is a critical substrate for the visuomotor representations necessary for functional object manipulation. This study investigates the componential nature of these representations within the supramarginal gyrus (SMG). Using functional MRI and a linear encoding model, the researchers demonstrate that neural activity in the SMG during object processing can be accurately predicted by a set of empirically defined kinematic synergies. The kinematic encoding model outperformed several control visual models—including deep convolutional neural networks (AlexNet, ResNet50, VGG16) and behavioral visual similarity metrics—in accounting for SMG variance. Furthermore, the amplitude of neural responses in the SMG was specifically modulated by the functional 'centrality' of manipulation to an object's identity, independent of low-level visual features. These results suggest that the SMG processes complex object-directed actions through the productive combination of a closed set of kinematic building blocks.

### 2025 — Visual processing of manipulable objects in the ventral stream is modulated by inputs from parietal action systems
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Frank E. Garcea, Emma L. Strawderman, William Burns, Matthew Cotroneo, Steven P. Meyers, Tyler Schmidt, Kevin A. Walter, Webster H. Pilcher, Bradford Z. Mahon

This study addresses the causal influence of dorsal visual pathway action systems on category-selective representations in the ventral temporal cortex (VTC). Utilizing Voxel-based Lesion-Activity Mapping (VLAM) in a sample of 109 neurosurgical patients with heterogeneous left-hemisphere lesions, the researchers show that damage to specific parietal structures—namely the anterior intraparietal sulcus and the supramarginal gyrus—causes a significant reduction in neural responses to tools in the medial fusiform gyrus. Contrastingly, parietal lesions did not modulate responses to non-manipulable categories such as places. Diffusion MRI connectometry further identified the descending portion of the left arcuate fasciculus as the primary white matter pathway mediating these dorsal-to-ventral interactions. These results demonstrate that the functional profile of the ventral stream is modulated by trans-cortical inputs from parietal action systems, supporting a model where motor-relevant information shapes visual object processing.

### 2024 — Strain concentration drives the anatomical distribution of injury in acute and chronic traumatic brain injury
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Adnan A. Hirad, Doran Mix, Arun Venkataraman, Steven P. Meyers, Bradford Z. Mahon

Tissue damage resulting from mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI) is known to concentrate in specific neuroanatomical loci, including the midbrain, cerebellum, and mesial temporal lobe. This study tests the hypothesis that these injury patterns are driven by the biomechanical susceptibility of different brain regions to strain loading. Using Magnetic Resonance Elastography (MRE) in healthy controls, the researchers developed whole-brain biomechanical vulnerability maps based on in vivo rheological properties. These maps were validated against a prospective cohort of mTBI patients using diffusion MRI collected at acute, sub-acute, and chronic intervals. The results demonstrate that regions exhibiting high strain concentration under MRE are the primary sites of structural injury as measured by diffusion MR. Decomposing the biomechanical drivers, the study shows that increased strain is caused by both disproportionate energy arrival at high-strain structures and the failure of those structures to effectively dissipate energy, establishing a causal mechanism for the anatomy of mTBI.
