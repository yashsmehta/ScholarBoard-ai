# Bruno Rossion

*human face recognition* — Université de Lorraine, CNRS Ingénierie moléculaire, cellulaire et physiopathologie (IMoPA); Service de Neurologie; Service de Neurochirurgie, Face Categorization Lab.

## Background

Bruno Rossion is a Research Director at the CNRS and Université de Lorraine specializing in the neuro-functional mechanisms of human face recognition and categorization. Rossion conducts research on the N170 electrophysiological component and case studies on acquired prosopagnosia to examine holistic face perception and the specialized nature of face processing in the human brain. Their methodology integrates multi-modal techniques including scalp EEG, functional MRI, and direct intracerebral recordings (sEEG) in patients with epilepsy. Rossion also applies fast periodic visual stimulation (FPVS), or frequency-tagging, as a tool for quantifying category-selective responses across the ventral occipito-temporal cortex in neurotypical and clinical populations.

## Papers

### 2026 — Concurrent category-selective neural activity across the ventral occipito-temporal cortex supports a non-hierarchical view of human visual recognition
*eLife*
Authors: Corentin Jacques, Jacques Jonas, Sophie Colnat-Coulbois, Bruno Rossion

This large-scale electrophysiological study evaluates the hierarchical organization of the human ventral visual stream using intracerebral recordings from 140 participants. By measuring category-selective responses to natural images across more than 11,000 recording sites, the authors characterized the spatiotemporal dynamics of high-frequency broadband gamma activity. While a postero-anterior gradient of increasing face-selectivity (abstraction) was observed along the ventral occipito-temporal cortex (VOTC), the onset and peak of these responses were largely concurrent (starting at approximately 100 ms) across posterior and anterior regions. The finding of simultaneous, correlated activity across 90 mm of cortical territory challenges traditional models of sequential processing stages and suggests a more integrated, non-hierarchical network for visual object recognition.

### 2025 — A tight relationship between BOLD fMRI activation/deactivation and increase/decrease in single neuron responses in human association cortex
*eLife · 2 citations*
Authors: Marie-Alphée Laurent, Corentin Jacques, Xiaoqian Yan, Pauline Jurczynski, Sophie Colnat-Coulbois, Louis Maillard, Steven Le Cam, Radu Ranta, Benoit R Cottereau, Laurent Koessler, Jacques Jonas, Bruno Rossion

This neurophysiological investigation addresses the coupling between Blood-Oxygen-Level-Dependent (BOLD) hemodynamic signals and cellular activity in the human association cortex. Using a frequency-tagging paradigm, the authors recorded single-unit (SU) activity and fMRI concurrently in the fusiform gyrus (FG) of patients undergoing epilepsy monitoring. They identified face-selective neurons in the lateral FG, where BOLD signals increased, and the medial FG, where BOLD signals decreased. The study demonstrates a precise correspondence where BOLD activations reflect selective increases in neuronal firing rates, while BOLD deactivations in the medial FG correlate with active suppressions of baseline spiking. Both excitatory and inhibitory neuronal responses occurred with similar mean onset latencies (~120 ms), providing robust evidence for a direct link between the direction of BOLD modulation and the sign of the underlying neural firing rate changes in the human brain.

### 2025 — The neural basis of face pareidolia with human intracerebral recordings
*Imaging Neuroscience · 3 citations*
Authors: Begüm Cerrahoğlu, Corentin Jacques, Diane Rekow, Jacques Jonas, Sophie Colnat-Coulbois, Stephanie Caharel, Arnaud Leleu, Bruno Rossion

The study explores the neurobiological mechanisms of face pareidolia by recording direct neural activity from the ventral occipito-temporal cortex (VOTC) in 44 participants. Using a frequency-tagging oddball paradigm, the researchers contrasted neural responses to biological faces, facelike objects (pareidolic stimuli), and non-face objects. Results indicate that face-selective responses to pareidolic stimuli are distributed across the same regions as biological face responses, including the previously under-sampled anterior temporal lobe. Despite lower response amplitudes compared to real faces, 89% of facelike object-selective contacts overlapped with human face-selective contacts and exhibited identical onset latencies. These findings suggest that face pareidolia is mediated by the primary face-categorization circuitry rather than through high-level cognitive feedback or distinct specialized regions.

### 2025 — A simple behavioral evaluation test of human face identity recognition with natural images validated with the case of prosopagnosia PS
*Scientific Reports*
Authors: Angélique Volfart, Caroline Michel, Bruno Rossion

This research develops and validates two streamlined behavioral instruments for assessing human face identity recognition (FIR) using naturalistic, 'ambient' images that incorporate variations in lighting, pose, and expression. The first test utilizes a three-alternative forced-choice (3AFC) familiarity selection task for famous identities, while the second employs an identity matching task with upright and inverted orientations. Validation data from neurotypical adults showed near-ceiling accuracy and clear face-inversion effects. Critically, testing the well-characterized brain-damaged patient PS revealed profound deficits in face-based identity tasks despite preserved performance with written names. These results confirm the tests' sensitivity and specificity for clinical and experimental evaluation of face-selective recognition impairments.

### 2024 — A robust neural index of automatic generalization across variable natural views of familiar face identities
*Visual Cognition · 2 citations*
Authors: Justine David, Laurent Koessler, Bruno Rossion

This study introduces an implicit electrophysiological marker for the brain's capacity to generalize identity across diverse naturalistic views of familiar faces. Using scalp EEG and a fast periodic visual stimulation (FPVS) adaptation paradigm, participants viewed 6 Hz sequences of natural face images. Every second stimulus involved an identity change (3 Hz), occurring within an adaptation context. The results revealed a robust neural response at the 3 Hz change frequency over the right occipito-temporal cortex, specifically reflecting the detection of identity change despite significant variability in head orientation, expression, and lighting. This neural signature provides an objective, task-independent measure of face identity recognition that circumvents response biases and explicit decision-making processes inherent in traditional behavioral tasks.
