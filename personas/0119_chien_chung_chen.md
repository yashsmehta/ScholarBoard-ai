# Chien-Chung Chen

*Visual neuroscience and psychophysics* — National Taiwan University, Department of Psychology, Visual Neuroscience Laboratory.

## Background

Chien-Chung Chen is a Distinguished Professor at National Taiwan University and directs the Visual Neuroscience Laboratory. Their research utilizes a multidisciplinary approach combining psychophysical experiments, functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), and computational modeling to investigate human visual processing. Chen’s work focuses on contrast discrimination, lateral inhibitory interactions in the primary visual cortex, and the neural representation of complex forms such as faces and symmetry. Currently, they examine the integration of spatial information and the role of feedback mechanisms in visual attention and object identification.

## Papers

### 2025 — Eye-movement patterns for perceiving bistable figures
*Journal of Vision · 2 citations*
Authors: Yi-Hsuan Hsu, Chien-Chung Chen

This psychophysical investigation evaluates the relationship between oculomotor behavior and the perception of bistable stimuli, such as the duck/rabbit figure and the Necker cube. By recording eye-fixation patterns alongside perceptual reversals, the study demonstrates that gaze location significantly modulates the likelihood of a specific percept. Specifically, the findings show that fixation shifts across different regions of interest (ROIs) occur prior to perceptual reversals, indicating that focal attention at specific image features drives the switch. The researchers also test the role of inhibition of return (IOR) and demonstrate that removing local saliency via masking increases reversal frequency, supporting a model where gaze shifts diminish responses at current fixations to facilitate new perceptual interpretations.

### 2024 — Visual field asymmetries in visual word form identification
*Vision Research · 4 citations*
Authors: Li-Ting Tsai, Kuo-Meng Liao, Chiun-Ho Hou, Yuh Jang, Chien-Chung Chen

This study maps visual acuity thresholds for identifying visual word forms (VWFs) across 21 locations in the visual field, ranging from foveal to 8° eccentricities, using traditional Chinese characters. The authors identify significant performance heterogeneities, specifically observing horizontal-vertical anisotropy (HVA)—where identification is superior along the horizontal compared to the vertical meridian—and vertical meridian asymmetry (VMA), where performance is better in the lower than the upper vertical meridian. By manipulating character contrast (10% to 80%), the study quantifies how contrast modulation interacts with eccentricity-dependent resolution limits. The results are analyzed in the context of the cortical magnification factor (M), suggesting that regional differences in word form processing are tied to the anatomical spacing of neural units in the visual cortex.

### 2024 — Integrated Gradient Correlation: a Dataset-wise Attribution Method
*arXiv (Preprint)*
Authors: Pierre Lelièvre, Chien-Chung Chen

The authors propose Integrated Gradient Correlation (IGC), a novel attribution method designed to enhance the interpretability of deep learning models at the dataset level rather than individual instances. Standard attribution methods often struggle with stable localization of information across large datasets. IGC addresses this by correlating dataset-wise attribution patterns with model prediction scores, enabling a direct summation over specific input components for region-specific analysis. The method is validated on neuroimaging data using the Natural Scenes Dataset (NSD), where IGC is employed to map image feature representations in the human visual cortex and estimate the receptive fields of neural populations. Results demonstrate that IGC effectively reveals model strategies that are physiologically coherent with the functional architecture of the brain.
