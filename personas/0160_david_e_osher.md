# David E Osher

*computational neuroscience and visual perception* — The Ohio State University, Department of Psychology, Cognition and Brain Circuitry Laboratory.

## Background

Osher is an Assistant Professor at The Ohio State University whose research examines the intersection of brain connectivity, functional specialization, and behavior through computational modeling. Their work utilizes machine learning and multimodal neuroimaging, such as functional and diffusion MRI, to develop 'connectivity fingerprints' that predict individualized neural responses and task-evoked activation. Osher investigates how connectivity patterns constrain information processing to understand the development of specialized regions for attention and high-level visual perception. Their research also extends to predicting cognitive outcomes and identifying neural markers for developmental disorders like dyslexia.

## Papers

### 2026 — Connectivity and function are coupled across cognitive domains throughout the brain
*Network Neuroscience · 1 citations*
Authors: Kelly J. Hiersche, Zeynep M. Saygin, David E. Osher

This study comprehensively evaluates the foundational hypothesis that the functional specialization of cortical regions is fundamentally determined by their extrinsic connectivity profiles. Utilizing high-resolution data from the Human Connectome Project (n = 1,018), the authors implemented ridge regression models to quantify the association between individualized resting-state functional connectivity (rs-fMRI) and task-evoked activation maps across 33 distinct cognitive domains, synthesized via the NeuroQuery meta-analytic framework. The findings reveal a robust and ubiquitous structure-function coupling across the entire brain, with the highest predictive fidelity observed in higher-order, domain-general systems compared to primary sensory regions. These results provide significant evidence for connectivity as a universal organizational principle of neural function and establish a normative reference for future research focused on individualized predictive modeling of brain activity.

### 2026 — Neural substrates of the oculomotor vergence network for concussion-related convergence insufficiency within the CONCUSS study
*NeuroImage: Clinical · 1 citations*
Authors: Yuxuan Zeng, Tamara S. Oechslin, Douglas E. Widmer, David E. Osher

This clinical neuroimaging study investigates the structural and functional correlates of convergence insufficiency (CI) in individuals following mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI). CI is an oculomotor impairment characterized by exophoria and reduced near-point convergence, which frequently impairs reading performance post-concussion. By examining patients from the CONCUSS cohort, the researchers analyzed white matter microstructural integrity and functional connectivity within the oculomotor vergence network. The analysis identified specific disruptions in the arcuate fasciculus and other associative white matter tracts, demonstrating that symptomatic post-concussive CI is associated with objective alterations in the neural pathways linking cortical vergence centers to brainstem motor nuclei. These findings offer potential biomarkers for assessing oculomotor recovery and the downstream neural consequences of concussion-related visual dysfunction.

### 2025 — Isolated Configuration: Holistic Processing Beyond the Realm of Faces
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Yuxuan Zeng, Zitong Lu, Ren E. Hentz, David E. Osher

This research challenges the domain-specific view of holistic processing as a mechanism exclusive to face perception. The authors developed novel, abstract stimuli designed to isolate configural information (the spatial arrangement of components) from featural information to test whether configural properties alone can drive holistic effects. Across four hallmark behavioral tasks—inversion, misalignment, part-whole, and composite effects—the study found that configural stimuli elicited robust holistic processing markers even with minimal training, whereas featural controls did not. Furthermore, deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) trained on these configural datasets exhibited human-like holistic processing patterns. The results suggest that holistic processing is a domain-general visual computation emerging from configural architectures, providing a computational framework for the origins of specialized face-selective systems.

### 2024 — Predicting high-level visual areas in the absence of task fMRI
*Scientific Reports · 10 citations*
Authors: M. Fiona Molloy, Zeynep M. Saygin, David E. Osher

The identification of category-selective functional regions of interest (fROIs) typically requires task-based fMRI localizers, which can be logistically challenging or costly. This paper introduces a method for predicting individualized fROIs in the ventral visual stream—including areas selective for faces, scenes, objects, and bodies—using only resting-state functional connectivity data. By training connectivity fingerprinting models on a large-scale dataset (HCP), the authors demonstrate that an individual’s unique connectome provides sufficient topographic information to accurately localize fROIs for high-level vision, as well as for language, motor, and working memory domains. This connectivity-based approach offers a precise and efficient alternative for functional mapping, particularly in clinical or pediatric populations where task compliance is limited.

### 2024 — Neural consequences of symptomatic convergence insufficiency: A small sample study
*Ophthalmic & Physiological Optics · 4 citations*
Authors: Yuxuan Zeng, Tamara S. Oechslin, Douglas E. Widmer, Marjean T. Kulp, Nicklaus Fogt, Andrew Toole, Steven Manning, David E. Osher

This study explores the neurobiological impact of symptomatic convergence insufficiency (CI), a common binocular vision disorder that manifests as exophoria and impaired convergence during near-work tasks. Utilizing fMRI and diffusion-weighted imaging in a cohort of symptomatic individuals, the researchers investigated how CI-related oculomotor deficits influence higher-order visual and cognitive networks. The results identified reduced functional recruitment in frontoparietal attention networks and microstructural alterations in white matter pathways during reading and visual search tasks. These findings suggest that the consequences of symptomatic CI extend beyond oculomotor discomfort, potentially affecting attentional allocation and reading efficiency by disrupting the neural circuits responsible for coordinating binocular input and cognitive processing.
