# Dirk Bernhardt-Walther

*High-level sensory perception* — University of Toronto, Department of Psychology, Bernhardt-Walther Lab.

## Background

Dirk Bernhardt-Walther is a Professor in the Department of Psychology at the University of Toronto and a Senior Member of the IEEE. Bernhardt-Walther’s research investigates the neural and computational foundations of high-level sensory perception, specifically how humans represent and categorize complex real-world scenes. By combining functional neuroimaging, psychophysics, and eye tracking with computer vision and machine learning, Bernhardt-Walther identifies mid-level visual features—such as contours and symmetry—that facilitate scene recognition and aesthetic preferences. Their work also includes the development of computational tools like the Mid-level Vision Toolbox to model how structural image properties influence human cognition and neural representations.

## Papers

### 2025 — Less is more: Aesthetic liking is inversely related to metabolic expense by the visual system
*PNAS Nexus · 2 citations*
Authors: Yikai Tang, William A. Cunningham, Dirk B. Walther

This study investigates the metabolic efficiency hypothesis of visual aesthetics using both artificial neural network modeling and human neuroimaging. Using a VGG19 architecture pretrained for object and scene recognition, the researchers demonstrated that images with higher aesthetic preference ratings consistently evoked sparser neural activity and lower metabolic costs (proxied by the total number of active neurons). This relationship was absent in randomly initialized networks. Subsequent fMRI experiments confirmed this inverse correlation in the human brain, showing that BOLD signal magnitude in early visual regions (V1, V2, V4) and high-level category-selective areas—specifically the Parahippocampal Place Area (PPA), Occipital Place Area (OPA), and Fusiform Face Area (FFA)—is negatively associated with subjective aesthetic liking. The findings suggest that aesthetic pleasure acts as an energy-conserving affect heuristic, reward-signaling visual stimuli that are metabolically efficient for the brain to process.

### 2025 — No evidence for a privileged role of global ensemble statistics in rapid scene perception: A registered replication attempt
*Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics*
Authors: Jiongtian Guo, Jay Pratt, Dirk B. Walther

This research presents a pre-registered replication of experiments asserting that global ensemble statistics (structureless texture information) are prioritized by the visual system for rapid scene perception compared to object perception. Utilizing a content-priming paradigm across multiple psychophysical experiments, the authors tested whether preserving only summary statistical information would selectively facilitate scene categorization. The results failed to replicate the purported 'global advantage' for scenes; instead, participants did not exhibit a privileged processing pathway for global properties over local structural features during rapid visual categorization. The study concludes that scene recognition does not rely on a unique, gist-first mechanism for ensemble statistics but rather follows a multi-scale processing pathway similar to that of object recognition.

### 2024 — Neural dissociation between computational and perceived measures of curvature
*Scientific Reports · 7 citations*
Authors: Oshin Vartanian, Delaram Farzanfar, Enric Munar, Martin Skov, Gregor Hayn-Leichsenring, Pik Ki Ho, Dirk B. Walther

To determine how the brain encodes curvature during aesthetic evaluation, this study reanalyzed fMRI data of participants making beauty judgments and approach-avoidance decisions regarding architectural scenes. The analysis revealed a significant neural dissociation: activity in the early visual cortex (V2-V3, BA 17) corresponds to computational curvature (geometrically extracted contour properties) regardless of the specific task or decision context. In contrast, the fusiform gyrus (BA 37) demonstrates sensitivity to perceived curvature—the subjective human judgment of curvilinearity—only when participants are actively engaged in aesthetic evaluation. These results demonstrate that while the low-level visual system tracks geometric invariants, higher-order cortical regions integrate subjective perceptual qualities to drive the hedonic appraisal of complex environments.

### 2024 — Aesthetic processing in neurodiverse populations
*Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews · 3 citations*
Authors: Zach Buck, Everan Michalchyshyn, Amna Nishat, Mikayla Lisi, Yichen Huang, Hanyu Liu, Arina Makarenka, Charles Puttcharnun Plyngam, Abigail Windle, Zhen Yang, Dirk B. Walther

This systematic review evaluates the neurobiological mechanisms of aesthetic preference through the lens of neurodiversity, expanding the field beyond traditional neuropsychological lesion studies to include psychotic disorders, anhedonia, depression, anxiety, and autism. The authors identify stable patterns of aesthetic bias across these populations and map them to dysfunctions within the mesocorticolimbic reward circuit, frontolimbic connectivity, and the default mode network (DMN). By synthesizing evidence across perceptual, emotional, and evaluative levels of cognition, the review proposes that specific symptomatologies modulate the neural architecture of value-based sensory processing. The paper argues that including neurodiverse populations is critical for developing a comprehensive theory of how the brain derives meaning and pleasure from visual information.

### 2024 — Organization of pRF size along the AP axis of the hippocampus and adjacent medial temporal cortex is related to specialization for scenes versus faces
*Cerebral Cortex · 11 citations*
Authors: Charlotte A. Leferink, Jordan DeKraker, Iva K. Brunec, Stefan Köhler, Morris Moscovitch, Dirk B. Walther

This study employs population receptive field (pRF) mapping to investigate the topographic and functional organization of the human medial temporal lobe (MTL). The researchers discovered a prominent gradient of pRF sizes along the anterior-posterior (AP) axis of the hippocampus, where anterior regions exhibit significantly larger receptive fields compared to the posterior sections. This scaling of visual sensitivity aligns with a gradient of category specialization: the larger pRFs in the anterior MTL correspond to areas specialized for global scene processing, while the smaller pRFs in the posterior MTL correspond to areas specialized for faces and fine-grained visual details. These findings establish that pRF scaling is a fundamental organizational principle linking the granularity of visual representations to high-level cognitive specialization for scenes and objects across the hippocampal-parahippocampal axis.
