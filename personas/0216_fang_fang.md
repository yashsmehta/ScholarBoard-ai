# Fang Fang

*Visual and cognitive neuroscience* — Peking University, School of Psychological and Cognitive Sciences, Lab of Vision and Cognitive Neuroscience.

## Background

Fang is a Professor of Brain and Cognitive Sciences and Vice President at Peking University, where they also serve as a Principal Investigator at the IDG/McGovern Institute for Brain Research. Their research investigates the neural mechanisms underlying visual perception, attention, and consciousness by integrating high-resolution fMRI, psychophysics, and transcranial electrical stimulation. Fang’s work includes the study of visual perceptual learning, cortical plasticity, and the representation of objects and faces in the human visual cortex. Their current research directions involve investigating neural prediction errors in visual reasoning and the role of subcortical nuclei in feature-based attentional control.

## Papers

### 2025 — Hierarchical Frequency Tagging Probe (HFTP): A Unified Approach to Investigate Syntactic Structure Representations in Large Language Models and the Human Brain
*The Thirty-ninth Annual Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS)*
Authors: Jingmin An, Yilong Song, Ruolin Yang, Nai Ding, Lingxi Lu, Yuxuan Wang, Wei Wang, Chu Zhuang, Qian Wang, Fang Fang

This study introduces the Hierarchical Frequency Tagging Probe (HFTP), a novel frequency-domain analytical framework designed to identify discrete neural components in both Large Language Models (LLMs) and the human brain (via intracranial recordings) that encode hierarchical syntactic structures. By applying this methodology to models including GPT-2, Gemma, and the Llama series, researchers demonstrated that artificial neural networks process syntax across layers in a manner that mirrors human brain processing hierarchies. Using representational similarity analysis (RSA), the study identified a significant alignment between LLM-based syntactic representations and neural activity in the left hemisphere, specifically within cortical regions traditionally associated with language processing. The findings provide a unified metric for comparing mechanistic syntax encoding across biological and artificial systems.

### 2025 — Frequency-dependent photic modulation of wakeful consolidation in visual learning
*Nature Communications*
Authors: Xin-Yue Yang, Chuyue Zhao, Zhentao Zuo, Aming Li, Huan Luo, Qing He, Fang Fang

This research investigates the modulation of wakeful consolidation in visual learning through repetitive visual stimulation (RVS) at specific temporal frequencies. Utilizing an orientation discrimination task (ODT), the study demonstrates that 10-Hz flickering grating RVS significantly enhances subsequent perceptual performance, whereas 1-Hz RVS induces a performance impairment. These effects were absent when training was omitted or when non-patterned uniform disks were utilized, suggesting the modulation is specific to early-stage sensory consolidation. Neurophysiological data from event-related potentials (ERPs) and magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) revealed that RVS-facilitated consolidation is characterized by enhanced N1 amplitudes and an elevated glutamate/GABA (excitation/inhibition) ratio in the early visual cortex (EVC). These findings implicate LTP-like synaptic plasticity as the underlying mechanism for the frequency-dependent photic modulation of learning consolidation during wakefulness.

### 2025 — Time Course of Orientation Ensemble Representation in the Human Brain
*Journal of Neuroscience*
Authors: Xizi Gong, Tao He, Qian Wang, Junshi Lu, Fang Fang

The study explores the neural mechanisms of orientation ensemble perception, specifically investigating whether the brain represents summary statistics through specialized processes or simple summation of individual item representations. Utilizing magnetoencephalography (MEG) and frequency-tagging EEG, the researchers employed multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) and inverted encoding models (IEM) to track the temporal evolution of orientation representations. Results indicate that a robust representation of the orientation ensemble emerges approximately 370 ms post-stimulus, predominantly localized within early visual areas. Crucially, IEM analysis showed that the ensemble representation is distinct from the linear summation of component orientations and is significantly correlated with individual behavioral reports of perceived average orientation. These findings suggest a gradual, specialized computational process in the human visual cortex that achieves accurate statistical summaries of complex visual scenes.

### 2024 — Human Visual Pathways for Action Recognition versus Deep Convolutional Neural Networks: Representation Correspondence in Late but Not Early Layers
*Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience*
Authors: Yujia Peng, Xizi Gong, Hongjing Lu, Fang Fang

This study evaluates the representational alignment between human visual pathways and two-stream deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) during the recognition of human actions in video sequences. Using fMRI-based decoding and representational similarity analysis (RSA), researchers compared neural responses across ventral and dorsal pathways with network activations in DCNN layers. The results demonstrate that, unlike the hierarchical alignment observed in static object categorization, DCNNs do not exhibit hierarchical representation correspondence with the human brain for dynamic action recognition. Discrepancies were particularly evident in early visual layers, though later DCNN layers showed greater representational similarity to the human visual cortex. These patterns persisted across both photorealistic avatars and point-light biological motion displays, indicating fundamental differences in how artificial and biological systems encode dynamic, multi-pathway visual information.

### 2024 — Time courses of brain plasticity underpinning visual motion perceptual learning
*NeuroImage*
Authors: Yongqian Song, Qian Wang, Fang Fang

This research utilizes magnetoencephalography (MEG) to characterize the high-resolution temporal sequence of neural changes induced by visual motion perceptual learning (VPL). Human participants were trained on a motion direction discrimination task, and MEG source reconstruction was applied to quantify plasticity in the early visual cortex (EVC) and V3A. The study found that VPL significantly increased neural selectivity and enhanced response amplitudes within the EVC. Furthermore, learning strengthened the feedforward connectivity from EVC to V3A, a change that manifested rapidly between 78 and 104 ms after stimulus onset. Subsequent neural modifications in response amplitude and selectivity occurred between 160 and 230 ms, correlating significantly with behavioral improvements in discrimination accuracy. The findings suggest that VPL modifies the visual hierarchy through both early-stage feedforward enhancement and later-stage refinement of neural representations.
