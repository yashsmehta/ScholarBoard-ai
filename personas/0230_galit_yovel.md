# Galit Yovel

*cognitive neuroscience of person recognition* — Tel Aviv University, School of Psychological Sciences, Person Recognition Lab.

## Background

Professor Galit Yovel researches the cognitive and neural architecture of high-level vision, specifically the mechanisms underlying face and person recognition. Yovel’s laboratory employs a multidisciplinary approach combining behavioral psychophysics, functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), and eye-tracking to investigate how perceptual, cognitive, and social systems integrate. A portion of their current work utilizes deep neural networks (DNNs) as computational models to contrast human visual processing with machine learning algorithms, aiming to characterize the representations of facial features and body identity. Through these methodologies, Yovel explores the functional organization of the face-selective cortex and the factors that enable recognition of familiar individuals.

## Papers

### 2025 — A developmentally inspired computational model of face recognition that learns continuously through generative memory replay
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Naphtali Abudarham, Galit Yovel

This study introduces a novel computational framework designed to bridge the architectural and ecological discrepancies between standard deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) and the human infant's ontogeny of face recognition. Unlike conventional models that utilize static batch training on expansive datasets with high within-identity variance, the proposed architecture employs a continuous learning paradigm utilizing generative memory replay. The system integrates three primary modules: an identity-encoding DCNN (Embedder), a generative Autoencoder, and a Long-Term Memory component. By simulating the gradual, low-variance visual exposure characteristic of early development—focusing on a limited number of caregivers—the model demonstrates high-fidelity identity classification and robustness against catastrophic forgetting. Quantitative results indicate that this biologically-constrained model achieves recognition performance parity with batch-trained DCNNs while better approximating the developmental trajectory of human face-space formation.

### 2025 — Concurrent emergence of view invariance, sensitivity to critical features, and identity face classification through visual experience: Insights from deep learning algorithms
*Journal of Vision*
Authors: Mandy Rosemblaum, Nitzan Guy, Idan Grosbard, Libi Kliger, Naphtali Abudarham, Galit Yovel

We utilized deep learning architectures to investigate the specific visual experience parameters required for the simultaneous development of view-invariant representations and sensitivity to diagnostic facial features. By systematically manipulating the network's training diet—specifically the balance between within-identity variability (multiple views per person) and between-identity diversity (total unique identities)—we assessed impact on identity verification performance and sensitivity to human-validated critical features. Our computational findings demonstrate that high-performance face recognition and view invariance do not emerge from raw image exposure alone but are contingent upon a training diet that replicates the high within-identity variability found in real-life experience. This suggests a shared representational basis where the emergence of sensitivity to identity-diagnostic features and view invariance are intrinsically coupled during the optimization of the face recognition system.

### 2024 — High-level visual cortex representations are organized along visual rather than abstract principles
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Adva Shoham, Rotem Broday-Dvir, Rafael Malach, Galit Yovel

This research addresses the controversial hypothesis that high-level visual cortex is organized according to abstract semantic or linguistic principles, as suggested by the predictive success of Large Language Models (LLMs) in modeling neural responses. Utilizing intracranial EEG (iEEG) recordings from human ventral temporal and fronto-parietal regions, we contrasted representational similarity structures across three input modalities: raw images, visual-textual descriptions (depicting pictorial content), and abstract-textual descriptions (conceptual/biographical data). Results from representational similarity analysis (RSA) show that while both visual and visual-textual representations accurately predict the neural response relational structure in high-level visual areas, abstract-textual embeddings show significantly lower alignment and fail to explain unique neural variance. These findings indicate that the observed vision-language alignment in human visual cortex is primarily driven by visually-grounded language rather than abstract conceptual organization.

### 2024 — Face- and body-selective brain regions enable clutter-tolerant representations of the face, body, and whole person
*The Journal of Neuroscience*
Authors: Libi Kliger, Galit Yovel

We investigated the neural mechanisms by which the primate visual system maintains robust representations of persons within complex, cluttered environments. Using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and a multi-stimulus paradigm, we measured neural responses to faces and bodies presented in isolation versus within cluttered scenes. Our results demonstrate that while early visual areas are significantly compromised by background clutter, high-level category-selective regions—specifically the fusiform face area (FFA) and extrastriate body area (EBA)—exhibit substantial clutter tolerance. Furthermore, we found that the presence of a body enhances the representation of a face within clutter (and vice versa), suggesting that interactions between adjacent face- and body-selective cortical modules facilitate the segregation of person-related signals from environmental noise. This synergy suggests a specialized cortical mechanism for holistic person perception that is optimized for real-world visual complexity.

### 2024 — Using deep neural networks to disentangle visual and semantic information in human perception and memory
*Nature Human Behaviour*
Authors: Adva Shoham, Idan Daniel Grosbard, Or Patashnik, Daniel Cohen-Or, Galit Yovel

This study employs a tripartite deep neural network (DNN) framework—consisting of vision-trained DCNNs, text-trained LLMs, and multimodal CLIP models—to computationally dissociate the visual and semantic constituents of human mental representations for familiar categories. By applying these models to behavioral data from perception and memory tasks, we quantified the relative contribution of each component to face and object recognition. Findings reveal a significant asymmetry: visual features dominate representational structures during active perception, whereas semantic/conceptual features show a significantly higher contribution during mental recall. Critically, we identified a unique, integrated visual-semantic component that is not captured by unimodal models alone, but is essential for both perception and memory. This suggests a representational shift where the high-dimensional visual details of perception are condensed into socially and semantically grounded identity concepts in memory.
