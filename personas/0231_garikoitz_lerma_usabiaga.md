# Garikoitz Lerma-Usabiaga

*Computational neuroimaging and vision* — Basque Center on Cognition, Brain and Language, Language and Memory Control group.

## Background

Garikoitz Lerma-Usabiaga is an Ikerbasque Research Fellow and Associate Group Leader at the Basque Center on Cognition, Brain and Language (BCBL). Lerma-Usabiaga’s research examines the neurobiological foundations of vision and literacy, focusing on the functional and structural architecture of the ventral occipitotemporal cortex. They utilize multimodal neuroimaging methodologies, including functional, quantitative, and diffusion MRI, alongside computational modeling techniques such as population receptive field (pRF) mapping. Their work also involves the development of reproducible software tools for automated tractography and the characterization of spatiotemporal dynamics within the human visual system across the lifespan.

## Papers

### 2025 — Biases in Volumetric Versus Surface Analyses in Population Receptive Field Mapping
*Human Brain Mapping*
Authors: David Linhardt, Michael Woletz, Pedro M. Paz-Alonso, Christian Windischberger, Garikoitz Lerma-Usabiaga

Population receptive field (pRF) mapping serves as a quantitative functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) technique to link specific cortical locations with visual field coordinates. A standard preprocessing step involves the projection of volumetric fMRI data onto the cortical surface, a process that typically entails spatial upsampling. This study investigated potential parameter biases introduced by this transformation using standardized analysis containers. Comparison of pRF maps derived from native volumetric data against those from upsampled surface data revealed a significant increase in pRF coverage within the central visual field across early visual areas (V1–V3). Computational analysis demonstrated that this bias is primarily mediated by cortical magnification—the nonlinear relationship between cortical distance and visual field eccentricity. These systematic discrepancies highlight the necessity of standardizing processing workflows to ensure the validity of cross-study comparisons in retinotopic mapping research.
