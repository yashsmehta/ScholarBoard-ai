# Hongjing Lu

*Computational vision and cognitive science* — University of California, Los Angeles, Departments of Psychology and Statistics, Computational Vision and Learning Lab.

## Background

Lu investigates the mechanisms of human learning and reasoning by developing computational models that emulate human-level intelligence. Their methodology integrates theories from psychology and statistics with tools from computer vision, machine learning, and computational neuroscience to perform behavioral and fMRI-based investigations. Lu’s research identifies two principles of human inference: the application of generic priors to guide learning from sparse data and the manipulation of structured representations for complex tasks. Current projects in their lab focus on biological motion perception, action recognition, and the computational underpinnings of causal and analogical reasoning in both biological and artificial systems.

## Papers

### 2026 — Network structure shapes consensus dynamics through individual decisions
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*
Authors: J. Hunter Priniski, Bryce Linford, Anna Hirschmann, Sai Krishna Venumuddala, Fred Morstatter, Nancy Rodriguez, P. Jeffrey Brantingham, Hongjing Lu

This study employs natural language processing and agent-based modeling to characterize the emergence and semantic trajectory of collective narrative beliefs within decentralized online social networks. By analyzing data from large-scale social network experiments, the researchers demonstrate that network topology fundamentally constrains the convergence of group consensus and the semantic orientation of communicative acts. An embedding-based narrative alignment metric revealed that fully connected networks prioritize the communication of causal antecedents and precursors, whereas locally connected topologies tend to emphasize event outcomes and effects. Although the presence of complex causal structures in source media can attenuate the influence of network topology by fostering deeper integration of prior background knowledge, structural connectivity remains a primary determinant of linguistic change. Participants in fully connected networks exhibited the most significant shift toward causal language in post-interaction narratives, suggesting that global connectivity optimizes the alignment of shared mental models regarding the causal structure of events.

### 2026 — Threat discrimination of real-world social interactions in schizotypal traits
*Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*
Authors: Akila Kadambi, Sophia Baia, Elinor Yeo, Hongjing Lu

This research investigates the behavioral and computational mechanisms underlying threat detection deficits across the schizophrenia spectrum, focusing on a nonclinical sample (N=161) with varying schizotypal traits. The study utilized naturalistic video stimuli of social interactions, processed via deep-learning-based rendering to selectively isolate or parse social contextual information. Findings indicate that individuals with high scores on schizotypal personality traits—specifically suspiciousness and odd beliefs—demonstrate a significant decline in threat discrimination sensitivity when presented with rich social context. Conversely, these individuals showed enhanced threat detection performance when social cues were abstracted to basic body kinematics. These results suggest that the social cognition impairments characteristic of the schizophrenia spectrum, and to a lesser extent the autism spectrum, are driven by an inability to effectively integrate complex social context, leading to a reliance on low-level motion cues for threat appraisal.

### 2025 — Enhancing visuospatial mapping in relational category learning
*Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition*
Authors: Andrew J. Lee, Keith J. Holyoak, Hongjing Lu

The acquisition of visual relational categories is theorized to depend on structured, compositional representations that explicitly encode role-filler bindings. However, the high cognitive overhead associated with analogical mapping over such representations often drives learners to rely on unstructured, feature-based exemplars, which significantly limits generalizability. This study investigated the efficacy of two visuospatial interventions—spatial categorization of exemplars and color-coded role highlighting—on the Synthetic Visual Reasoning Test (SVRT). Through a series of experiments, the researchers found that spatially segregating positive and negative instances significantly accelerated concept discovery, particularly when learning was scaffolded with an easy-to-hard problem sequence. In contrast, color-based role highlighting only facilitated learning when it provided unambiguous and nonredundant cues for structural alignment. The results suggest that specific environmental scaffolds can mitigate cognitive load and maintain the engagement of compositional reasoning mechanisms during relational category acquisition.

### 2025 — Evidence from counterfactual tasks supports emergent analogical reasoning in large language models
*PNAS Nexus*
Authors: Taylor W. Webb, Keith J. Holyoak, Hongjing Lu

This paper evaluates the emergent analogical reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) against the hypothesis that their performance is a byproduct of training data mimicry. The authors employed 'counterfactual' reasoning tasks, including letter-string analogies with permuted alphabets and a novel 'Digit Matrices' task using permuted numerical sequences, designed to minimize similarity to existing training corpora. While standard GPT-4 performance degraded on these zero-shot counterfactual tasks, the study reveals that augmenting the model with a code execution interface—enabling precise list indexing and counting—allows it to achieve human-level proficiency. These results demonstrate that the previously reported failures of LLMs on counterfactual benchmarks are likely due to auxiliary processing bottlenecks, such as counting errors, rather than a lack of fundamental relational reasoning capacity. The findings support the existence of a generalized, emergent ability for abstract structural mapping in modern LLMs that extends beyond statistical retrieval.

### 2025 — Self-Awareness from Whole-Body Movements
*Journal of Neuroscience*
Authors: Akila Kadambi, Gennady Erlikhman, Micah Johnson, Martin M. Monti, Marco Iacoboni, Hongjing Lu

This study utilizes human neuroimaging and multivariate pattern analysis to investigate the neural mechanisms underlying whole-body self-recognition from dynamic point-light displays. Despite the absence of first-person visual experience and the sparsity of biological motion signals, individuals can accurately identify their own actions. The researchers employed general linear modeling and representational similarity analysis (RSA) on fMRI data to dissociate brain regions specialized for self-processing. Results identified a distinct frontoparietal network, specifically including motor-related regions, that exhibits increased activation and finer-grained representations when observers view their own movements compared to those of others. These findings support common coding theories of action, suggesting that self-action recognition is mediated by a matching process that integrates visual input with prior motor and proprioceptive experience, rather than relying solely on visual template matching.
