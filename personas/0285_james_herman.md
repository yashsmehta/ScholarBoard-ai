# James Herman

*subcortical-cortical visual interactions* — University of Pittsburgh, Department of Ophthalmology, Laboratory of Visual Cognition.

## Background

Herman investigates how primate visual perception and cognitive functions such as attention and decision-making emerge from coordinated activity between the cerebral cortex and subcortical regions including the superior colliculus and basal ganglia. Their research characterizes the neural mechanisms of learning-driven behavioral changes using a combination of high-density extracellular electrophysiology, psychophysics, and targeted neuronal perturbations. Recently, Herman’s work has integrated computational modeling, including recurrent vision transformers, to analyze the relationship between biological and artificial intelligence in how populations of neurons govern specific cognitive tasks.

## Papers

### 2025 — Independent encoding of salience, value, and attention in primate superior colliculus
*Journal of Vision*
Authors: Matthew Murawski, James Herman

The study investigates how the primate superior colliculus (SC) integrates signals of physical salience, reward value, and goal-directed attention. While some theories suggest the SC acts as a unified priority map, neural recordings from macaques performing a spatially cued orientation-change detection task alongside a saccade task revealed that these three factors exert independent influences. Single-neuron analysis and population-level linear classifiers demonstrated that representations of salience, reward, and attention do not generalize across categories, indicating a modular rather than a unified encoding scheme. These findings support a model where the SC flexibly routes information to guide distinct overt orienting and covert attentional behaviors based on behavioral context.

### 2025 — A recurrent vision transformer shows signatures of primate visual attention
*arXiv (cs.CV)*
Authors: Jonathan Morgan, Badr Albanna, James P. Herman

This research introduces a Recurrent Vision Transformer (Recurrent ViT) that incorporates a capacity-limited spatial memory module and recurrent feedback to simulate top-down attentional control in primates. Unlike standard feedforward transformers, this architecture allows stored information and current visual inputs to jointly determine attention allocation. Trained via reinforcement learning on a cued orientation-change detection task, the model replicates key biological signatures of attention, including heightened detection accuracy and decreased reaction times for validly cued stimuli. Analysis of self-attention maps shows dynamic spatial prioritization and reactivation of cues before expected changes, while internal weight perturbations produce performance shifts comparable to those observed in the macaque frontal eye fields and superior colliculus.

### 2024 — A Neural Network Model of Visual Attention Integrating Biased Competition and Reinforcement Learning
*bioRxiv (Preprint)*
Authors: Jonathan Mark Morgan, Badr Albanna, James P Herman

The authors present a neural network model of visual attention (NNMVA) that bridges the gap between AI self-attention and biological mechanisms of biased competition. The model utilizes self-attention from Vision Transformers (ViTs) to simulate competition between representations, coupled with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) for working memory and an actor-critic reinforcement learning framework. By training the system solely on reward feedback during a cued orientation-change detection task, the model develops behavioral patterns typical of non-human primates, such as reaction time benefits and accuracy improvements for attended locations. The study demonstrates that reward-driven learning signals can sufficiently organize top-down feedback to guide attentional prioritization in a manner consistent with subcortical and cortical circuits.
