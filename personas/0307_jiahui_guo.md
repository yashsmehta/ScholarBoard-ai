# Jiahui Guo

*cognitive and computational neuroscience* — The University of Texas at Dallas, Department of Psychology, School of Behavioral and Brain Sciences, Cognitive & Computational Neuroscience (CoCo-Neuro) Lab.

## Background

Jiahui Guo is an Assistant Professor at The University of Texas at Dallas whose research explores the neural mechanisms of high-level perception, with an emphasis on face recognition and social cognition. Guo’s work utilizes naturalistic stimuli and functional neuroimaging to map individualized brain topographies and investigate how specialized cortical regions support visual tasks. By integrating computational modeling, deep neural networks, and neuropsychological assessments of populations such as those with prosopagnosia, Guo aims to develop models of human functional brain organization and its behavioral outcomes.

## Papers

### 2026 — Predicting Individualized Functional Topography in Developmental Prosopagnosia
*bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory)*
Authors: Ian Abenes, Guo Jiahui

Abstract Functional localizer scans have long served as the classic method for mapping individualized functional topographies, but they require dedicated scan time and can be difficult to implement in neuropsychological populations. Previous work has shown that individualized functional topographies can be estimated with high fidelity in typical participants using hyperalignment, but it remains unknown whether this approach generalizes to populations with functional deficits. Here, we tested this question in developmental prosopagnosia (DP), a neuropsychological condition characterized by severe face recognition impairments. Using two independent datasets that included both DP and control participants, we estimated individualized category-selective functional topographies from independent participants using hyperalignment derived from either a task-based scan or a naturalistic movie-viewing scan. Across datasets, whole-brain correlations and searchlight analyses showed that predicted topographies were highly similar to topographies estimated from participants’ own localizer data, especially in cortical areas with strong category-selective responses. Hyperalignment successfully recovered idiosyncratic features of category-selective topographies and consistently outperformed anatomical alignment. Importantly, predictions generalized across groups, such that individualized topographies in DPs could be estimated from control participants and vice versa. In addition, predicted topographies preserved the reduced face selectivity in DPs previously reported in the literature. These findings support a hyperalignment-based framework for estimating individualized functional topographies in neuropsychological populations without requiring separate localizer scans, and provide a foundation for integrating existing datasets to study the underlying neural basis in DP and other atypical populations.

### 2024 — Shared connectome and organization in the human cortex irrespective of sensory experience
*Cognitive Computational Neuroscience (CCN)*
Authors: Jiahui Guo, Francesca Setti, Ma Feilong, Davide Bottari, Maria Ida Gobbini, Pietro Pietrini, Emiliano Ricciardi, James V. Haxby

This study employs a naturalistic movie-viewing paradigm (using adapted audio and video versions of 101 Dalmatians) to examine the extent to which the functional architecture of the high-level human cortex is dependent on sensory experience. By applying connectivity hyperalignment to functional MRI data from congenitally blind and deaf individuals, we demonstrate that category-selective functional topographies, particularly in the ventral visual pathway, develop and organize into a shared latent space regardless of prior visual or auditory input. Our findings reveal that the fundamental connectome organization associated with specialized processing is largely conserved across modalities, suggesting that the cortical infrastructure for high-level representations can emerge independently of specific sensory experiences.

### 2023 — Modeling naturalistic face processing in humans with deep convolutional neural networks
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*
Authors: Jiahui Guo, Ma Feilong, Matteo Visconti di Oleggio Castello, Samuel A. Nastase, James V. Haxby, M. Ida Gobbini

This research evaluates the efficacy of deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) as computational models for the neural and cognitive processing of dynamic, naturalistic faces. Using a large-scale dataset of 707 naturalistic face video clips, we conducted representational similarity analysis (RSA) across DCNN layers, human behavioral arrangements, and fMRI-measured neural responses in face-selective regions. Results indicate that late-intermediate DCNN layers effectively capture categorical face attributes (e.g., age, gender, expression) that align with human behavioral and neural geometries. Conversely, the final fully connected layers, optimized for identity discrimination, show significantly weaker correlations with human brain and behavioral data, suggesting that current DCNN architectures do not fully replicate the mechanisms underlying human face individuation and dynamic feature processing.

### 2023 — A normative database for specialized functional topographies
*eLife*
Authors: Jiahui Guo, Ma Feilong, Samuel A. Nastase, James V. Haxby, M. Ida Gobbini

We present a novel computational framework and a normative database designed to estimate individualized functional brain topographies using naturalistic movie stimuli. By utilizing connectivity hyperalignment (CHA) to map idiosyncratic functional architectures into a shared high-dimensional model, we can predict participant-specific, category-selective regions (e.g., face, place, and body-selective areas) from resting-state or naturalistic viewing data. Our methodology demonstrates that movie-based predictions achieve high fidelity, frequently exceeding the reliability of traditional functional localizer scans. This database serves as a resource for precisely localizing specialized cortical regions in individual brains across diverse datasets, facilitating the study of fine-grained functional organization and individual differences.

### 2023 — The Individualized Neural Tuning Model: Precise and generalizable cartography of functional architecture in individual brains
*Imaging Neuroscience*
Authors: Ma Feilong, Samuel A. Nastase, Jiahui Guo, Yaroslav O. Halchenko, M. Ida Gobbini, James V. Haxby

We introduce the Individualized Neural Tuning (INT) model, a generative framework that characterizes brain functional architecture through a shared stimulus matrix and individual-specific neural tuning matrices. This model enables the precise mapping of individualized functional topographies, including category-selective regions and retinotopic maps, from high-dimensional neural responses to naturalistic stimuli. Our experiments demonstrate that the INT model accurately predicts individualized, fine-grained response patterns to novel stimuli and effectively quantifies inter-individual variability in functional organization. The model provides a robust solution for aligning idiosyncratic brain topographies into a common representational space while preserving individual-specific functional granularity.
