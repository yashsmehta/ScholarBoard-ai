# Jocelyn Faubert

*Perceptual-cognitive neuroscience* — Université de Montréal, School of Optometry, Faubert Lab.

## Background

Jocelyn Faubert is a professor of visual perception and cognitive neuroscience at the Université de Montréal, where they direct a multidisciplinary research laboratory focused on human performance. Faubert’s work investigates the neural mechanisms of sensory-cognitive integration, specifically how aging, neurodevelopmental conditions, and traumatic brain injuries affect the processing of complex dynamic scenes. They developed the NeuroTracker system, a 3D multiple-object tracking technology used to assess cognitive load in clinical settings and modify mental performance in athletes. Their research methodology integrates psychophysical testing with immersive virtual reality environments, such as CAVE systems and EON Icube technology, to connect fundamental vision science with real-world applications.

## Papers

### 2025 — Critical role of EEG signals in assessment of sex-specific insights in neurological diagnostics via machine learning approach
*Scientific Reports*
Authors: Mohammad-Javad Darvishi-Bayazi, Mohammad Sajjad Ghaemi, Irina Rish, Jocelyn Faubert

This study utilizes artificial deep neural networks to evaluate the detectability of biological sex within electroencephalogram (EEG) signals using extensive datasets of over 12,000 subjects. Applying balanced accuracy (BAcc) as a robust performance metric, the research demonstrates that although sex-related neural patterns are highly consistent and transferable across diverse populations, they play a negligible role as confounders in the machine-learning-based detection of neurological pathologies. The results suggest that sex-specific biomarkers, while prominent in EEG data, do not significantly bias the diagnostic accuracy for pathological states, providing critical insights for the development of equitable AI models in neurological disease prediction.

### 2024 — The nature of multi-channel color perception from three photodetector types with wide overlapping spectral sensitivity bands
*Optics Continuum*
Authors: Vasile Diaconu, Jocelyn Faubert

This research introduces a neurophysiologically inspired physical model for the de-multiplexing of signals from three types of broadband retinal photodetectors (L, M, and S cones) characterized by significant spectral overlap. The framework implements fundamental retinal signal processing principles, specifically the contrast between photoreceptor signals and the ON/OFF antagonistic responses of the neural network, to redirect inputs into separate chromatic channels corresponding to red, green, blue, yellow, and white perception. The proposed method offers a computationally feasible alternative to traditional additive/subtractive models for simulating human-like color coding in spectroscopy and multichannel imaging applications.

### 2024 — “Extended Descriptive Risk-Averse Bayesian Model” a More Comprehensive Approach in Simulating Complex Biological Motion Perception
*Biomimetics*
Authors: Khashayar Misaghian, J. Eduardo Lugo, Jocelyn Faubert

A hierarchical Bayesian simulation model is proposed to represent the human visual system's dorsal pathway for processing complex biological motion, specifically the perception of soccer kick directions. By incorporating a disremembering strategy and mutual inhibition mechanisms between motion pattern detectors, the model successfully simulates the psychometric functions, angular thresholds, and reaction times of expert athletes. The findings underscore the critical role of rotational optic flow and motion cues, rather than dynamic form, in enabling the robust perception of biological movement from sparse point-light stimuli, matching human behavioral data with high precision.

### 2024 — Immediate fall prevention: the missing key to a comprehensive solution for falling hazard in older adults
*Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience*
Authors: Khashayar Misaghian, Jesus Eduardo Lugo, Jocelyn Faubert

This perspective article identifies a critical gap in geriatric fall prevention, arguing that current solutions lack the necessary predictive technology to intercept falls before impact. The authors propose a framework based on the breakdown of high-level predictive cognitive mechanisms and dorsal pathway motion processing in the aging brain. By utilizing computational models of biological motion perception, the paper advocates for the development of real-time anticipation-interception systems. Such technologies aim to close the gap between life expectancy and wellness expectancy by shifting the focus from post-fall detection to proactive, immediate prevention of hip fractures and related traumas.

### 2024 — Gender comparison of perceptual-cognitive learning in young athletes
*Scientific Reports*
Authors: Isabelle Legault, Jocelyn Faubert

This study examines sexual dimorphism in the baseline performance and learning rates of perceptual-cognitive skills in 72 young athletes and non-athletes using a 3D-Multiple Object Tracking (3D-MOT) paradigm. Results indicate that while male athletes achieve significantly higher initial absolute speed thresholds, the learning curves across five training weeks are similar for both sexes and both athlete/non-athlete status. The findings confirm that participating in competitive sports is associated with superior perceptual-cognitive function and suggest that while males may have higher temporal resolution of attention at baseline, the capacity for cognitive enhancement through training is consistent across genders.
