# Katharina Dobs

*cognitive computational neuroscience* — Justus Liebig University Giessen, Institute for Computer Science, Visual Cognitive Computational Neuroscience (VCCN) Lab.

## Background

Dobs is a Professor of Applied Computer Science at Justus Liebig University Giessen and directs the Visual Cognitive Computational Neuroscience (VCCN) Lab. Their research integrates deep learning architectures with human behavioral and neuroimaging data to investigate the computational principles underlying visual recognition and the functional organization of the cortex. Dobs has conducted studies demonstrating that brain-like functional specialization, such as face-selective regions, emerges in artificial neural networks optimized for specific visual tasks. Their current research, supported by an ERC Starting Grant and a LOEWE Start Professorship, focuses on developing models to explain the organization of the human visual system into specialized domains.

## Papers

### 2026 — Prior Scene Context Shapes the Neural Dynamics of Face Detection
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Sule Tasliyurt-Celebi, Daniel Kaiser, Katharina Dobs

This study characterizes how prior contextual expectations derived from scene layout modulate the temporal dynamics of face detection using electroencephalography (EEG) and multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA). Observers performed a face detection task on natural scenes containing a single face, which were preceded by either a faceless preview of the same scene or a neutral control. Behavioral results demonstrated significant facilitation in face localization speed and accuracy in the presence of contextual previews. Neurally, face-location decoding revealed that prior scene context allows representations to emerge significantly earlier, suggesting a rapid modulation of sensory-driven processing. Furthermore, time-frequency analyses identified a sustained preview advantage in induced oscillatory activity across alpha, beta, and gamma bands, while the absence of context elicited delayed, compensatory neural signals. These results indicate that even rapid face perception reflects an interaction between incoming sensory input and temporally distinct contextual prior knowledge.

### 2025 — Diverse Visual Experience Promotes Integrated Representations and Mitigates Bias in Deep Neural Networks for Face Perception
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Elaheh Akbari, Katharina Dobs

This research investigates the computational origins of the Other-Race Effect (ORE) by analyzing how the diversity of training exposure influences representational geometry in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). By training CNNs on exclusively Asian faces, exclusively White faces, or a diverse mix of both, and comparing their performance to human behavioral data, the authors found that homogeneous training exposure spontaneously generates ORE-like recognition biases. Representational similarity analysis (RSA) revealed that these biases are associated with a lack of cross-group representational overlap. Conversely, networks trained on diverse face sets developed an integrated representational geometry with balanced discriminative performance across groups, which most accurately captured human behavioral benchmarks. These findings suggest that human recognition biases emerge as a consequence of optimizing neural representations to the specific statistical distributions of familiar groups and provide a framework for mitigating bias through diverse visual experience.

### 2025 — Human-like face pareidolia emerges in deep neural networks optimized for face and object recognition
*PLOS Computational Biology*
Authors: Pranjul Gupta, Katharina Dobs

The authors explore the functional basis of face pareidolia—the perception of face-like patterns in inanimate objects—using task-optimized deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) as computational models. They tested whether pareidolia emerges from the visual system's simultaneous optimization for fine-grained face identification and generic object categorization. Comparing CNN internal representations to human behavioral signatures and neural dynamics measured via magnetoencephalography (MEG), the study found that only dual-task networks (trained on both face and object tasks) aligned with human neural responses. These networks initially represented pareidolia stimuli as similar to real faces, relying on shared features like eyes for classification. This suggests that face pareidolia is not a perceptual error but a byproduct of a broadly tuned face detection mechanism designed to support robust, high-performance face recognition within complex real-world environments.

### 2025 — Multidimensional feature tuning in category-selective areas of human visual cortex
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Leonard E. van Dyck, Martin N. Hebart, Katharina Dobs

This work proposes a unifying framework for the functional organization of the human high-level visual cortex by reconciling categorical and dimensional perspectives. Utilizing a data-driven Bayesian non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) of fMRI voxel responses to thousands of natural images, the authors identified multiple interpretable representational dimensions within well-known category-selective areas (FFA, EBA, and PPA). They found that even highly selective voxels remain tuned to multiple, partially overlapping dimensions that encode subcategory features and cross-category distinctions. These dimensions form sparse subclusters within selective areas while also contributing to globally distributed feature-based maps across the visual cortex. The results suggest that category selectivity emerges from sparse tuning to a limited set of high-level dimensions, providing a more multifaceted and comprehensive account of how the brain encodes complex visual information than traditional categorical models.

### 2024 — Artificial intelligence meets body sense: task-driven neural networks reveal computational principles of the proprioceptive pathway
*Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy*
Authors: Leonard E. van Dyck, Frank Bremmer, Katharina Dobs

This article highlights the application of task-driven deep neural networks to decode the computational logic of the proprioceptive system. By training artificial networks to solve motor control and musculoskeletal tasks, the authors show how these models can predict the complex neural dynamics of proprioceptive processing in both the cuneate nucleus and the primary somatosensory cortex. The study emphasizes that these normative models not only achieve high predictive accuracy of neural activity but also provide insights into the specific format of limb-state representations required for movement execution. Additionally, the paper discusses the translational potential of these biologically inspired encoders for neuroprosthetics, suggesting that understanding the goal-driven organization of proprioception is critical for restoring sensory feedback in brain-machine interfaces. This work advocates for the expansion of NeuroAI frameworks to include somatosensory and motor domains.
