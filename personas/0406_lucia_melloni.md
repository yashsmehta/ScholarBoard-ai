# Lucia Melloni

*neural mechanisms of consciousness* — Max Planck Institute for Empirical Aesthetics, Predictive Brain Department, Research Center One Health Ruhr, Neural Circuits, Consciousness, and Cognition Research Group.

## Background

Lucia Melloni is a cognitive neuroscientist who investigates the neural and computational foundations of consciousness, perception, and memory. Melloni’s research examines how the brain utilizes predictive mechanisms to interpret sensory input and shape subjective experience through a multimodal approach that integrates invasive electrocorticography (ECoG), non-invasive MEG/EEG, and computational modeling. They coordinate large-scale adversarial collaborations designed to empirically arbitrate between competing theories of consciousness as part of an open science framework. Their work also explores the neuroethological aspects of experience, examining how memory and prediction contribute to the continuity of conscious life in real-world contexts.

## Papers

### 2025 — Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness
*Nature*
Authors: Cogitate Consortium, Oscar Ferrante, Urszula Gorska-Klimowska, Simon Henin, Rony Hirschhorn, Aya Khalaf, Alex Lepauvre, Ling Liu, David Richter, Katarina Bendtz, Tara Ghafari, Dorottya Hetenyi, Jay Jeschke, Csaba Kozma, Ole Jensen, Gabriel Kreiman, Huan Luo, Melanie Boly, Stanislas Dehaene, Christof Koch, Giulio Tononi, Michael Pitts, Liad Mudrik, Lucia Melloni

This large-scale adversarial collaboration implemented a theory-neutral framework to adjudicate between the Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT). Utilizing a sample of 256 human subjects, the study employed multimodal neuroimaging (fMRI, MEG, and iEEG) to test preregistered, divergent predictions regarding the neural correlates of conscious visual perception. Results demonstrated that conscious content could be decoded from visual, ventrotemporal, and inferior frontal cortices. Sustained neural activity in posterior regions correlated with stimulus duration, while content-specific frontal-visual synchronization was observed. These findings provide partial evidence for both theories while simultaneously challenging fundamental assumptions, specifically IIT’s claim regarding sustained posterior synchrony and GNWT’s emphasis on late prefrontal ignition events.

### 2025 — Towards a neuroethological approach to consciousness
*Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences*
Authors: Yuranny Cabral-Calderin, Julio Hechavarria, Lucia Melloni

This article proposes a shift in consciousness research towards a neuroethological framework, applying Tinbergen’s four questions: mechanism, ontogeny, adaptive value, and phylogeny. The authors argue that current research is excessively human-centric, primarily focusing on verbal reports in neurotypical adults. By shifting toward an ethological perspective, researchers can investigate consciousness as an evolutionary continuum shaped by ecological pressures. The review emphasizes the importance of integrating neurophysiological metrics with naturalistic behavior to explore conscious states in non-verbal populations, including infants and non-human animals, thereby providing a more robust foundation for identifying the universal biological principles of subjective experience.

### 2025 — “What” and “when” predictions jointly modulate speech processing
*Journal of Neuroscience*
Authors: Ryszard Auksztulewicz, Ozan Bahattin Ödül, Saskia Helbling, Ana Böke, Drew Cappotto, Dan Luo, Jan Schnupp, Lucía Melloni

This study examined the hierarchical integration of 'what' (content) and 'when' (temporal) regularities in auditory speech processing using magnetoencephalography (MEG). By manipulating statistical regularities at syllable and pseudoword levels, the authors demonstrated that temporal predictability at slower time scales selectively enhanced mismatch responses to deviant pseudowords, whereas faster scales modulated responses to syllables. Source reconstruction localized these hierarchical interactions to a shared frontoparietal network. Dynamic causal modeling (DCM) revealed that the integration of congruent content and timing predictions is mediated by hierarchical gain modulation within this distributed cortical network, rather than distinct anatomical levels for each temporal scale.

### 2025 — Unpacking the complexities of consciousness: theories and reflections
*Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*
Authors: Liad Mudrik, Melanie Boly, Stanislas Dehaene, Stephen M. Fleming, Victor A.F. Lamme, Anil K. Seth, Lucia Melloni

This review synthesizes the current state of consciousness science, focusing on the lack of convergence among major neuroscientific theories. The authors analyze prominent frameworks, including Global Neuronal Workspace (GNW) and Integrated Information Theory (IIT), against the backdrop of results from recent large-scale adversarial collaborations. The paper identifies critical points of theoretical divergence, such as the necessity of prefrontal involvement and the nature of neural synchronization. By highlighting the limitations of current empirical benchmarks, the authors propose a roadmap for more precise theory testing, calling for the development of quantitative, theory-neutral protocols to bridge the gap between competing explanatory models of subjective experience.

### 2025 — What you saw a while ago determines what you see now: The effect and temporal dynamics of awareness priming on implicit behavior
*Cognition*
Authors: Zefan Zheng, Darinka Trübutschek, Shuyue Huang, Yongchun Cai, Lucia Melloni

Using Continuous Flash Suppression (CFS), this study investigated awareness priming, defined as the modulation of current perceptual access by previous conscious experiences. The researchers found that prior conscious exposure to words significantly increased both the subjective visibility and objective discrimination of stimuli presented at the threshold of awareness. A distinct temporal dissociation was identified: short-term history (1-10 previous trials) primarily boosted discrimination accuracy, while long-term history (10-30 trials) selectively enhanced subjective visibility. These findings suggest that areas lower in the cortical hierarchy support objective performance over short intervals, whereas higher-order regions with broader temporal integration windows mediate conscious awareness over extended periods.
