# Masataka Sawayama

*material perception and vision science* — University of Tokyo, Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, Information Media Environment Laboratory (IME Lab).

## Background

Masataka Sawayama is an Associate Professor specializing in visual 'Shitsukan' (material perception), investigating how humans recognize complex surface qualities such as gloss, wetness, and translucency. Sawayama’s work integrates human psychophysics with computational modeling and deep neural networks to identify the image cues and neural mechanisms involved in material recognition. Their research also explores the intersection of computer vision and cognitive neuroscience, including the development of brain-decoding models and the analysis of semantic representations in the visual cortex.

## Papers

### 2026 — Human Visual Perception of Shitsukan
*Shitsukan—Understanding and Manipulating Material and Quality Perception (Springer Nature)*
Authors: Shin'ya Nishida, Masataka Sawayama

This research chapter provides a comprehensive synthesis of human Shitsukan (material and quality) perception, focusing on the computational and psychophysical mechanisms used to estimate properties like gloss, wetness, and translucency. The authors argue that the estimation of these physical attributes is an inherently ill-posed problem because image formation depends on the nonlinear interplay of geometry, illumination, and material. They propose that the human visual system circumvents these complexities by relying on diagnostic image statistics and intensity gradient information rather than full physical reconstruction. The work reviews evidence for how different layers of the visual hierarchy—from early luminance histograms to higher-level semantic understanding—contribute to our robust experience of material qualities in natural environments.

### 2025 — Revisiting Color Efficient Coding through Material Perception
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Masataka Sawayama

This study investigates the efficient coding of natural scenes by examining the redundancy in cone signals and early visual transformations. While classical theories emphasize the decorrelation of luminance and color signals (e.g., via PCA), the author notes that object material properties introduce significant redundancy between these channels due to complex optical interactions. Utilizing a median cut algorithm derived from computer graphics, this research proposes a novel decomposition method that enhances processing efficiency while isolating diagnostic features for material versus geometry. Behavioral psychophysical experiments indicate that color reduction via this algorithm significantly impairs material classification performance but leaves object recognition largely unaffected, implying that geometric structure remains accessible even in low-bit visual representations.

### 2024 — Correspondence between Monkey Visual Cortices and Deep Neural Networks reveals task-specific representations of material
*eNeuro*
Authors: Takahiro Kawabe, Masataka Sawayama

This study explores the alignment between neural representations in the macaque visual system and deep neural networks (DNNs) specifically for material perception. By analyzing multi-unit activity from the inferior temporal (IT) cortex and V4 while monkeys performed material-related tasks, and comparing these signals to various DNN architectures (including vision-language and purely vision-based models), the researchers evaluated how task-specific objectives influence representational similarity. The findings demonstrate that models trained with high-level semantic objectives or multimodal supervision exhibit higher correspondence with cortical responses in IT than models focused on low-level image features. This suggests that the primate ventral stream organizes material information based on task-relevant features and semantic category rather than simple visual statistics.
