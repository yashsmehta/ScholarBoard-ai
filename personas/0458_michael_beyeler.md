# Michael Beyeler

*Bionic vision and neuroengineering* — University of California, Santa Barbara, Department of Computer Science and Department of Psychological & Brain Sciences, Bionic Vision Lab.

## Background

Michael Beyeler is an Associate Professor at the University of California, Santa Barbara, and directs the Bionic Vision Lab. Their research integrates computational neuroscience, artificial intelligence, and neuroengineering to develop visual neuroprostheses for individuals with blindness. Beyeler focuses on building biophysically grounded models of neural stimulation and using virtual reality testbeds to optimize prosthetic interfaces. This work utilizes multimodal data and machine learning to investigate and augment human visual perception.

## Papers

### 2025 — Gamification Enhances User Engagement and Task Performance in Prosthetic Vision Testing
*medRxiv*
Authors: Lily M. Turkstra, Byron A. Johnson, Arathy Kartha, Gislin Dagnelie, Michael Beyeler

This study evaluates the influence of gamification on the psychophysical assessment of retinal prosthesis recipients (n=3 Argus II users). By comparing standard clinical paradigms with gamified versions of circle localization and motion direction discrimination tasks—incorporating multimodal elements such as scoring, adaptive difficulty, and affectively framed auditory feedback—the researchers quantified performance via response precision and accuracy. Findings demonstrate that gamification significantly improves localization performance and reduces angular error, likely by mitigating the cognitive fatigue associated with repetitive testing; however, effects on motion processing were heterogeneous, suggesting that the impact of engagement-enhancing features is task-dependent and mediated by the intrinsic cognitive load of the perceptual task.

### 2025 — Look, predict, intercept: Visual exposure seeds model-based control in moving-target interception
*PsyArXiv*
Authors: Lauren Tenaya Eckhardt, Anvitha Akkaraju, Tori N. LeVier, Justin M. Kasowski, Michael Beyeler

This research investigates the control strategies underlying the interception of moving targets that undergo temporary occlusion using immersive virtual reality paradigms. In two experiments involving ambulatory interception (N=18) and shooting tasks (N=41), the study tests a hybrid control hypothesis. Participants maintained interception accuracy significantly above chance following target disappearance, with curved motor trajectories emerging only when visual information was withdrawn. These results support a two-stage, effector-invariant interception strategy where an initial period of visual exposure (approximately 1–1.5 s) is sufficient to seed a predictive internal model that sustains control in the absence of continuous optic flow input.

### 2025 — Mouse vs. AI: A Neuroethological Benchmark for Visual Robustness and Neural Alignment
*arXiv*
Authors: Marius Schneider, Joe Canzano, Jing Peng, Yuchen Hou, Spencer LaVere Smith, Michael Beyeler

To address the gap between the robust generalization of biological vision and the vulnerability of reinforcement learning (RL) agents to distributional shifts, this work introduces a bioinspired benchmark based on a visually guided foraging task in a 3D Unity environment. Agents are evaluated on two complementary tracks: (1) Visual Robustness, assessing behavioral generalization across ecologically realistic visual perturbations (e.g., fog, illumination changes); and (2) Neural Alignment, quantifying the ability of an agent's internal representations to predict large-scale calcium imaging recordings from over 19,000 neurons in mouse primary visual cortex (V1) and higher visual areas (HVA) via linear readouts.

### 2025 — Assistive technology use in domestic activities by people who are blind
*Scientific Reports*
Authors: Lily M. Turkstra, Tanya Bhatia, Alexa Van Os, Michael Beyeler

This mixed-methods study characterizes the informational needs and adaptive strategies of legally and totally blind individuals (N=16) during the performance of instrumental activities of daily living (iADLs) at home. Through semi-structured interviews and behavioral observations, the authors identify a prevalent preference for low-tech tactile systems over digital alternatives and a hierarchical problem-solving framework where digital assistant applications are typically utilized as a last resort. Key barriers to technology adoption include accessibility regressions in software updates and the challenge of precise object localization, suggesting that future assistive neurotechnologies must better integrate with the multimodal and user-specific nature of domestic workflows.

### 2024 — Aligning Visual Prosthetic Development With Implantee Needs
*Translational Vision Science & Technology*
Authors: Lucas Nadolskis, Lily M. Turkstra, Ebenezer Larnyo, Michael Beyeler

This qualitative study evaluates the translational efficacy of current bionic eye technologies by reconciling the lived experiences of six visual prosthesis recipients (Argus II and Orion) with the expectations of six leading researchers. Thematic and statistical analyses reveal a significant discrepancy between predicted and actual device utilization, with implantees using their devices less frequently than researchers anticipated due to usability, reliability, and functional limitations. Implantees emphasized a critical demand for enhanced spatial resolution, intelligent software integration for task-specific assistance, and greater independent utility, advocating for a shift toward user-centered design in the development of next-generation visual neuroprostheses.
