# Miguel Eckstein

*computational vision and visual attention* — University of California, Santa Barbara, Department of Psychological & Brain Sciences, Vision and Image Understanding Laboratory.

## Background

Miguel Eckstein is a Professor at the University of California, Santa Barbara, and directs the Vision and Image Understanding Laboratory. Eckstein’s research integrates psychophysics, computational modeling, and neuroimaging techniques—including fMRI and EEG—to investigate the mechanisms of human visual search, attention, and face perception. Their work includes modeling human performance in complex tasks, such as medical image interpretation. Eckstein also develops bio-inspired computer vision systems to bridge the gap between human and machine intelligence.

## Papers

### 2025 — Emergent neuronal mechanisms mediating covert attention in convolutional neural networks
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*
Authors: Sudhanshu Srivastava, William Yang Wang, Miguel P. Eckstein

This study characterizes the internal representations of deep neural networks that develop human-like covert spatial selection through task optimization in noisy environments. By analyzing the activation patterns of over one million individual units, the authors identify emergent 'location-opponent' neurons that exhibit differential excitatory and inhibitory tuning to cued versus uncued locations. These findings suggest that the neurophysiological hallmarks of attention, such as gain modulation and spatial suppression, can arise as an emergent property of hierarchical visual architectures learning to maximize target detection sensitivity without explicit top-down feedback or specialized attention modules.

### 2025 — The psychophysics of dynamic gaze-following saccades during search
*Journal of Vision*
Authors: Srijita Karmakar, Miguel P. Eckstein

This research investigates the oculomotor control mechanisms and psychophysical constraints of gaze-following in naturalistic, dynamic visual search tasks. Utilizing video stimuli of social scenes, the authors quantify how foveal gaze information and peripheral scene context integrate to guide saccadic targeting. The results demonstrate that foveal gazer cues significantly reduce the spatial error of the first saccade towards goals at large eccentricities (~9 degrees) and that observers execute predictive, anticipatory saccades based on gazer head velocity before the movement's completion. The study elucidates how the brain integrates social cues with spatial constraints to optimize search efficiency in complex environments.

### 2025 — Convolutional neural network model observers discount signal-like anatomical structures during search in virtual digital breast tomosynthesis phantoms
*Journal of Medical Imaging*
Authors: Aditya Jonnalagadda, Bruno B. Barufaldi, Andrew D. A. Maidment, Miguel P. Eckstein

This work evaluates the performance of convolutional neural network (CNN) model observers in replicating the search behavior of expert radiologists during digital breast tomosynthesis (DBT) interpretation. Unlike standard linear model observers, which are limited by local noise statistics, the CNN models demonstrated the ability to 'discount' or suppress responses to normal anatomical structures that possess target-like features (signal-like masses). This selective discounting allows CNN observers to more accurately predict human search sensitivity and specificity, providing a computationally efficient framework for optimizing 3D medical imaging protocols through the lens of human-centered computer vision.

### 2024 — Greater benefits of deep learning-based computer-aided detection systems for finding small signals in 3D volumetric medical images
*Journal of Medical Imaging*
Authors: Devi S. Klein, Srijita Karmakar, Aditya Jonnalagadda, Craig K. Abbey, Miguel P. Eckstein

The authors assess the differential performance impact of deep learning computer-aided detection (CADe) systems on human search efficiency across 2D versus 3D volumetric medical imaging modalities. Utilizing digital breast tomosynthesis (DBT) and synthetic 2D mammography, the study finds that AI assistance provides a significantly greater boost to human d' in 3D search. This result is linked to the CADe's ability to mitigate intrinsic human search limitations, specifically the under-exploration of large 3D volumes caused by the foveated nature of the visual system and the high cognitive load associated with navigating through slice-based volumetric data.

### 2024 — Emergent human-like covert attention in feedforward convolutional neural networks
*Current Biology*
Authors: Sudhanshu Srivastava, William Yang Wang, Miguel P. Eckstein

This paper presents evidence that feedforward convolutional neural networks (CNNs) spontaneously exhibit behavioral signatures of human covert attention when trained to optimize target detection in noise. Despite lacking explicit attentional feedback mechanisms, the networks demonstrate significant cueing effects, including increased sensitivity (d') and faster convergence at validly cued locations. The findings propose a normative account for the existence of covert attention, suggesting it is not necessarily a specialized neural module but a computational property that emerges as a solution to maximizing perceptual sensitivity in hierarchical visual systems.
