# N Apurva Ratan Murty

*cognitive computational neuroscience* — Georgia Institute of Technology, School of Psychology, Murty Lab.

## Background

N Apurva Ratan Murty is an Assistant Professor of Cognition and Brain Science at the Georgia Institute of Technology and directs the Murty Lab. Murty’s research focuses on the intersection of biological vision and artificial intelligence to map the neural algorithms and codes associated with visual intelligence. By combining high-field functional MRI (3T/7T) and human electrophysiology with deep neural networks and generative AI, Murty develops models such as TopoNets to characterize the functional organization of the brain. Their work investigates category-selective brain regions and the neurobiological foundations of perceptual disorders, including agnosia and prosopagnosia.

## Papers

### 2025 — TopoNets: High Performing Vision and Language Models with Brain-Like Topography
*International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR)*
Authors: Mayukh Deb, Mainak Deb, N. Apurva Ratan Murty

Cortical neurons in the mammalian brain exhibit topographic organization, where units with similar functional tuning are spatially clustered. Standard artificial neural networks (ANNs) typically lack this organization, and previous attempts to enforce topography have often resulted in significant task performance degradation. This work introduces TopoLoss, a novel training objective that facilitates the emergence of spatially organized functional representations without substantial loss in predictive accuracy. TopoNets, trained using this framework, demonstrate brain-like characteristics such as localized feature processing, reduced representational dimensionality, and increased parameter efficiency. Furthermore, these models show superior alignment with neural activity in the visual and language cortices compared to non-topographic baselines.

### 2025 — Targeted perturbations reveal brain-like local coding axes in robustified, but not standard, ANN-based encoding models
*arXiv preprint*
Authors: Nikolas McNeal, N. Apurva Ratan Murty

While artificial neural network (ANN) encoding models have become the standard for predicting human fMRI responses, their reliability under targeted stimulus manipulations has not been extensively tested. We demonstrate that contemporary encoding models are unexpectedly fragile, exhibiting high sensitivity to small, imperceptible stimulus perturbations that reveal unreliable local coding directions. We show that a model's sensitivity to adversarial probes is a more effective discriminator for biological alignment than standard prediction accuracy. Critically, we find that robustified models—trained with adversarial robustification techniques—develop stable, brain-like local coding axes and spatially localized feature sensitivity that better capture the functional organization of the primate visual system.

### 2025 — End-to-end Topographic Auditory Models Replicate Signatures of Human Auditory Cortex
*arXiv preprint*
Authors: Haider Al-Tahan, Mayukh Deb, Jenelle Feather, N. Apurva Ratan Murty

The human auditory cortex is topographically organized, featuring smooth tonotopic maps for frequency and modular regions specialized for speech and music. However, standard auditory neural networks do not inherently replicate this spatial structure. We present TopoAudio, a suite of end-to-end topographic auditory models trained using a cortical wiring-length constraint. These models maintain competitive task performance and fMRI predictivity while reproducing hallmark biological features, including smooth tonotopic and amplitude-modulation maps in early layers and spatially clustered selectivity for music and speech in higher-order layers. Our results suggest that spatial smoothness constraints represent a domain-general organizing principle for achieving biologically grounded representations in artificial systems.

### 2024 — Linking faces to social cognition: The temporal pole as a potential social switch
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*
Authors: Alish Dipani, Nikolas McNeal, N. Apurva Ratan Murty

This work explores the neural architecture of the human face-processing network and its integration with social cognition. Based on recent fMRI findings, we distinguish between the roles of the perirhinal cortex (PR) and the temporal pole (TP). While the PR is involved in face-specific perceptual identification, the TP serves as a critical person-identity node that links facial representations to social semantic knowledge and Theory of Mind networks. We argue that the TP acts as a functional 'social switch,' facilitating the transition from visual face recognition to complex social inference. This framework suggests that the TP is a key evolutionary adaptation for managing the high-dimensional social information processed by the primate brain.

### 2024 — Small-scale adversarial perturbations can expose differences between predictive encoding models of human fMRI responses
*Proceedings of UniReps: Workshop on Unifying Representations in Neural Models (PMLR)*
Authors: Nikolas McNeal, Mainak Deb, Apurva Ratan Murty

ANN-based encoding models of the human visual system often achieve similar levels of voxel-wise predictive accuracy, making it difficult to distinguish between competing models using standard metrics. We propose adversarial sensitivity as a novel probe to characterize the local representational geometry and biological alignment of these models. By applying small-magnitude stimulus perturbations, we reveal significant vulnerabilities in contemporary encoding frameworks and demonstrate that robustness to adversarial probes is more effective than prediction score at identifying brain-like representations. Our results show that incorporating robustness constraints into encoding models yields internal features that more closely align with the localized coding strategies observed in the human visual cortex.
