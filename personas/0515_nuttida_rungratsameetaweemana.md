# Nuttida Rungratsameetaweemana

*computational and systems neuroscience* — Columbia University, Department of Biomedical Engineering, Systems Intelligence Lab.

## Background

Nuttida Rungratsameetaweemana is an Assistant Professor of Biomedical Engineering at Columbia University and the principal investigator of the Systems Intelligence Lab. Their research investigates the neural computations and coding schemes that facilitate adaptive cognitive processes, such as flexible decision-making, memory maintenance, and contextual information processing. By integrating biologically constrained recurrent neural networks with experimental data from human intracranial recordings and functional neuroimaging, Rungratsameetaweemana characterizes the mechanisms of goal-directed information transformation across the cortical hierarchy. Their work demonstrates that early sensory regions play an active, task-dependent role in shaping neural representations to support flexible behaviors.

## Papers

### 2025 — Neural heterogeneity shapes the temporal structure of human working memory
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Daria Kussovska, Robert Kim, Nuttida Rungratsameetaweemana

The maintenance of information within working memory (WM) has traditionally been linked to persistent population activity, though continuous neural discharge is energetically demanding and often inconsistent with observed cortical heterogeneity. Utilizing single-trial spiking analysis of 902 neurons from 21 neurosurgical patients, we demonstrate that WM maintenance is supported by transient, burst-like coordinated activity rather than sustained firing. Cross-temporal decoding revealed localized generalization, with decoding accuracy scaling with the width of temporal windows, suggesting that apparent persistence emerges from temporally interleaved activity across the neural ensemble. A feature-based cell-type classifier identified pyramidal neurons as the primary carriers of content-specific information during burst events, while interneurons were strongly modulated by memory load and behavioral variables, indicating a flexible and metabolically efficient circuit substrate for information retention.

### 2025 — Feedback promotes efficient-coding while attenuating bias in recurrent neural networks
*arXiv*
Authors: Holly Kular, Robert Kim, John Serences, Nuttida Rungratsameetaweemana

While environmental regularities facilitate efficient-coding in human decision-making, non-uniform stimulus statistics often induce deleterious representational biases in artificial neural networks, compromising generalization. We investigated this trade-off using a three-layer hierarchical continuous-time recurrent neural network (ctRNN) trained on biased stimulus distributions. We found that the networks preferentially allocated representational resources to high-probability stimuli across the hierarchy, in line with efficient-coding principles. Critically, the selective ablation of top-down feedback connections magnified these biases and suppressed the representation of rare stimuli. These results suggest that while hierarchical models exploit statistical regularities for efficiency, feedback pathways are essential for regulating representational bias and maintaining the robust processing of low-probability inputs.

### 2025 — Brain2Model Transfer: Training sensory and decision models with human neural activity as a teacher
*arXiv*
Authors: Tomas Gallo Aquino, Victoria Liu, Habiba Azab, Raissa Mathura, Andrew J Watrous, Eleonora Bartoli, Benjamin Y Hayden, Paul Sajda, Sameer A Sheth, Nuttida Rungratsameetaweemana

Transfer learning typically utilizes large pre-trained artificial models, but human neural activity provides a potentially superior source of low-dimensional, abstract representations optimized for sensorimotor tasks. We introduce Brain2Model Transfer (B2M), a framework where human electrophysiological data collected during sensory and decision-making tasks serves as a teacher signal for training artificial neural networks. We propose two strategies: Brain Contrastive Transfer, aligning network activations with neural signals via a contrastive objective, and Brain Latent Transfer, projecting task-specific latent dynamics via supervised regression. In applications including memory-based decision-making in RNNs and scene reconstruction in VAEs, B2M-guided models exhibited significantly faster convergence and higher predictive accuracy than those trained in isolation, demonstrating the utility of biological representations for enhancing artificial learning.

### 2025 — Categorization dynamically alters representations in human visual cortex
*Nature Communications*
Authors: Margaret M. Henderson, John T. Serences, Nuttida Rungratsameetaweemana

Human cognitive flexibility relies on the dynamic reconfiguration of selective attention to extract abstract features from complex stimuli based on varying goals. We examined the adaptation of sensory representations in the human visual cortex as participants categorized novel shapes according to different linear and non-linear boundaries. Using fMRI and representational similarity analysis (RSA), we found that task-specific categorization rules dynamically reorganize population-level codes to prioritize goal-relevant features. These findings demonstrate that sensory representations are not static but are actively sharpened by top-down constraints, providing a mechanistic link between task-dependent feedback and the flexible mapping of sensory inputs onto abstract behavioral categories.

### 2024 — Disinhibitory signaling enables flexible coding of top-down information
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Tomas G. Aquino, Robert Kim, Nuttida Rungratsameetaweemana

The ability to map identical sensory inputs to different actions based on context is a hallmark of cognitive flexibility, yet its circuit-level implementation remains poorly understood. We investigated the role of disinhibitory signaling within recurrent neural networks (RNNs) performing context-dependent decision tasks. Our results indicate that disinhibitory pathways—specifically those involving interneuron-mediated gating—facilitate the flexible representation of top-down information by modulating the gain of specific neural populations. This mechanism enables the dynamic reorganization of representational manifolds, preventing interference between conflicting task mappings. We conclude that disinhibition serves as a fundamental computational substrate for hierarchical control and the implementation of context-aware behavioral flexibility.
