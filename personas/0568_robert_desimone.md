# Robert Desimone

*visual attention and perception* — MIT, Department of Brain and Cognitive Sciences, Desimone Lab.

## Background

Robert Desimone serves as the Director of the McGovern Institute for Brain Research and the Doris and Don Berkey Professor at MIT, where they investigate the neural mechanisms of selective attention and executive control. Desimone’s research characterizes how the brain manages information overload by utilizing synchronous neuronal firing to amplify relevant sensory signals while suppressing background distractions. By employing electrophysiology, MEG, and optogenetics in primate models, Desimone identified circuits in the prefrontal and extrastriate cortex that coordinate top-down attentional focus. These findings inform the development of treatments for brain disorders such as schizophrenia and autism.

## Papers

### 2025 — Stimulus representations in visual cortex shaped by spatial attention and microsaccades
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*
Authors: Karthik Srinivasan, Eric Lowet, Bruno Duarte Gomes, Robert Desimone

This study demonstrates that fixational eye movements, specifically microsaccades (MSs), are fundamentally linked to the neural mechanisms of covert spatial attention in the primate visual system. Utilizing multiunit recordings from rhesus macaques (Macaca mulatta) across cortical areas V4 and IT, as well as the lateral pulvinar, the researchers found that the typical firing rate enhancements associated with spatial attention are only engaged following a microsaccade directed toward the attended stimulus. If the initial MS after an attentional cue is directed away from the target, the expected gain modulation is delayed until the subsequent target-directed MS occurs. Furthermore, stimulus decoding accuracy and the precision of tuning curves in V4 were significantly improved during intervals following target-directed MSs compared to those following MSs directed away from the target. These findings imply that microsaccades do not merely correlate with attention but serve to coordinate and amplify object processing in extrastriate cortex, providing a neural basis for active vision during periods of fixation.

### 2025 — Whole-brain effective connectivity of the sensorimotor system using 7T fMRI with electrical microstimulation in non-human primates
*Progress in Neurobiology*
Authors: Rui Xu, Narcisse P. Bichot, Atsushi Takahashi, Robert Desimone

To elucidate the causal pathways of the primate sensorimotor system, the authors combined ultra-high-field (7 Tesla) functional MRI with focal electrical microstimulation (EM-fMRI) in rhesus macaques. By stimulating functionally identified regions of the primary somatosensory cortex and measuring brain-wide BOLD responses, they mapped the effective connectivity of local cortical sites to distal subcortical and cortical targets. The results demonstrate a highly specific topographic organization of sensorimotor signals, revealing isomorphic mappings between the lateral prefrontal cortex and major processing domains. The findings suggest that effective connectivity, rather than just spontaneous correlations, provides a quantitative measure of the directional and causal interactions necessary for coordinated sensorimotor control. The study also validates an MRI-compatible, 3D-printable microdrive system for precise electrode positioning in high-field environments.

### 2025 — A ubiquitous spectrolaminar motif across independent studies, including Mackey et al.'s own data
*bioRxiv (Preprint)*
Authors: Alex James Major, Ahmed Abdaltawab, Jessica M. Phillips, Tian Wang, Eric Kenji Lee, Maxwell J. Lichtenfeld, Chandramouli Chandrasekaran, Yuri B. Saalmann, Alexander Maier, Earl K. Miller, André M. Bastos, Robert Desimone, Diego Mendoza-Halliday

This work serves as a comprehensive rebuttal and technical validation of the 'spectrolaminar motif' discovered in previous research (Mendoza-Halliday et al., 2024), which suggests a canonical distribution of local field potential (LFP) power across cortical layers. Addressing criticisms regarding the tool derived from this motif (FLIP/vFLIP), the authors introduce vFLIP2, an enhanced algorithm for frequency-based layer identification. By analyzing diverse datasets from multiple independent research groups, including those from critics, the study confirms that high-frequency gamma power peaks in supragranular layers while alpha-beta power peaks in infragranular layers across the primate cortex. The consistent localization of these gradients relative to histologically defined layers supports the existence of a universal electrophysiological signature for the six-layered anatomical motif of the neocortex, facilitating more accurate layer identification in laminar electrophysiology recordings.

### 2024 — Dissociable neuronal substrates of visual feature attention and working memory
*Neuron*
Authors: Diego Mendoza-Halliday, Haoran Xu, Frederico A. C. Azevedo, Robert Desimone

In this investigation, the authors challenge the hypothesis that visual working memory (WM) and feature-based attention rely on the same neural substrates within the primate brain. By performing simultaneous laminar recordings in macaque monkeys across a hierarchy of areas including MT, MST, LIP, and the lateral prefrontal cortex (LPFC), they characterized the neural signatures of motion-direction WM and feature-based attention. The results reveal that while both processes recruit similar cortical regions, they are represented by distinct neuronal populations and rely on different circuit mechanisms. Specifically, whereas feature attention consistently modulates the gain of sensory-driven responses, WM representations are maintained through distinct firing patterns in different subpopulations. Optogenetic inactivation of the posterior LPFC selectively impaired attentional modulation without abolishing the underlying WM representation, demonstrating a functional dissociation between the selection of incoming sensory information and the maintenance of internal representations.

### 2024 — Functional architecture of cerebral cortex during naturalistic movie watching
*Neuron*
Authors: Reza Rajimehr, Haoran Xu, Asa Farahani, Simon Kornblith, John Duncan, Robert Desimone

This research provides a comprehensive map of the human cerebral cortex's functional organization by analyzing high-resolution fMRI data from 176 subjects during 60 minutes of naturalistic movie watching. Utilizing a data-driven clustering approach, the authors identified a stable macro-architecture consisting of 24 functional networks, each specialized for distinct sensory or cognitive processes such as language, social interactions, and visual feature extraction. The study highlights the existence of three domain-general executive control networks that exhibit a robust 'push-pull' relationship with domain-specific sensory areas; these networks were most active during transitions between movie segments and least active when feature-specific networks were highly engaged. By mapping the cortex under naturalistic conditions rather than constrained tasks, the study reveals a more unified and ecologically relevant parcellation scheme that captures the dynamic interplay of large-scale brain systems.
