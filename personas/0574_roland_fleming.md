# Roland Fleming

*Visual perception of materials and objects* — Justus Liebig University Giessen, Department of Psychology, Fleming Lab.

## Background

Roland W. Fleming holds the Kurt Koffka Professorship of Experimental Psychology at Justus Liebig University Giessen and serves as the Executive Director of the Center for Mind, Brain and Behavior, a collaborative research center involving the Universities of Marburg and Giessen and TU Darmstadt. Fleming’s research investigates how the human brain estimates physical and functional properties of objects, such as 3D shape, gloss, and material elasticity, from retinal images. To study these processes, their lab combines psychophysical experiments, computer graphics, and unsupervised deep learning to model the visual system’s method of disentangling environmental cues. Fleming’s recent work focuses on the neural representations of core material dimensions and the computational mechanisms that enable humans to predict physical events and guide motor actions.

## Papers

### 2026 — No free lunch with the binding problem
*Trends in Cognitive Sciences*
Authors: Giacomo Aldegheri, Roland W. Fleming

This work presents a fundamental re-evaluation of the visual binding problem, challenging the traditional assumption that explicit feature-integration mechanisms are required for coherent object perception. Drawing on the success of deep neural networks in visual processing tasks without dedicated binding architectures, the authors argue that the visual system exploits the rich statistical regularities and inherent feature correlations present in natural scenes. The paper suggests that these statistical dependencies allow the brain to bypass the classical binding problem, implying that the specialized neural processes historically postulated in cognitive science may be less necessary for functional vision than previously thought.

### 2025 — Material fingerprinting: predicting human perception of material appearance through psychophysical analysis and neural networks
*Royal Society Open Science*
Authors: Jiri Filip, Filip Dechterenko, Filipp Schmidt, Jiri Lukavsky, Jan Kotera, Veronika Vilimovska, Roland W. Fleming

The authors introduce the 'visual fingerprint,' a perceptual representation framework that links high-dimensional image measurements of materials to sixteen human-understandable appearance attributes. Using a psychophysical dataset of 347 real-world materials and over 110,000 human ratings, the study maps perceptual attributes across diverse categories including wood and fabrics. By integrating multimodal deep neural network features (CLIP) with a multi-layer perceptron, the researchers developed a predictive model capable of accurately reproducing human judgments of appearance and similarity using as few as two image frames, providing an intuitive and interpretable approach to material representation for digital environments.

### 2025 — Orientation fields predict human perception of 3D shape from shading
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*
Authors: Celine Aubuchon, Romain Vergne, Steven A. Cholewiak, Benjamin Kunsberg, Daniel Holtmann-Rice, Steven W. Zucker, Roland W. Fleming

This study identifies orientation fields, derived from the responses of orientation-selective populations in the primary visual cortex (V1), as the primary image measurement driving human 3D shape perception from shading. The researchers establish a robust statistical correspondence between image orientation fields and 3D surface curvature properties that generalizes beyond ideal specular reflections to common shaded objects. Through psychophysical experiments, they demonstrate that orientation-based models accurately predict specific human shape illusions occurring under varied lighting and the robustness of shape perception against image modifications that disrupt luminance-based cues, resolving long-standing questions about the neural inputs for shape-from-shading.

### 2025 — Gloss discrimination: Toward an image-based perceptual model
*Journal of Vision*
Authors: Jacob R. Cheeseman, James A. Ferwerda, Takuma Morimoto, Roland W. Fleming

This research investigates the visual discriminability of surface gloss across variations in illumination, shape, and viewpoint to establish a perceptual standard for material appearance. Using human paired-comparison tasks and maximum likelihood difference scaling (MLDS), the study quantifies how distal scene variables influence the statistics of proximal image data and subsequent perceptual sensitivity. Findings reveal that human gloss discrimination is highly consistent across individuals and is effectively predicted by a straightforward visual differences predictor (VDP). This framework allows for the estimation of visual discriminability bounds for surfaces across a wide range of realistic viewing conditions, independent of physical specular reflectance parameters.

### 2025 — Core dimensions of human material perception
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*
Authors: Filipp Schmidt, Martin N. Hebart, Alexandra C. Schmid, Roland W. Fleming

The authors employ a large-scale data-driven approach to identify the foundational representational dimensions of material perception in humans. Analyzing thousands of similarity judgments using a multimodal deep neural network (CLIP), they identified 36 interpretable dimensions that organize material perception across a vast array of visual appearances. This low-dimensional embedding accurately captures single-trial similarity judgments and reduces the complexity of relating image features to external behavior. The study demonstrates that these data-driven dimensions provide a bridge between low-level image statistics and high-level semantic categories, allowing for comprehensive modeling of how the visual system represents the physical and functional properties of materials.
