# Sashank Varma

*Computational modeling of mathematical cognition* — Georgia Institute of Technology, School of Interactive Computing and School of Psychology, Cognitive Architecture Lab.

## Background

Sashank Varma is a cognitive scientist who integrates human experimentation with computational modeling to study forms of human intelligence. Varma investigates the cognitive mechanisms underlying mathematical and computational thinking, including the processing of abstract geometric and topological concepts. Their research examines the 'cognitive alignment' between humans and machine learning models, utilizing computer vision and large language models to explore whether AI developmental trajectories mirror human cognitive progression. This work aims to define the neuroarchitecture of perception and reasoning while addressing the educational implications of cognitive learning principles.

## Papers

### 2026 — Are More Tokens Rational? Inference-Time Scaling in Language Models as Adaptive Resource Rationality
*arXiv preprint (arXiv:2602.10329)*
Authors: Zhimin Hu, Riya Roshan, Sashank Varma

This research investigates whether resource rationality—the optimization of performance under inherent computational constraints—emerges as a general property of inference-time scaling in Large Language Models (LLMs). The study employs a novel Variable Attribution Task (VAT) to systematically manipulate task complexity by varying the number of candidate variables and logical trials (e.g., XOR and XNOR functions). Performance is compared between instruction-tuned (IT) models that utilize chain-of-thought prompting and Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) optimized via reinforcement learning for strategic reasoning path discovery. The findings indicate that both model types demonstrate an emergent transition from brute-force search to human-like analytic strategies as task complexity increases, even in the absence of explicit cost-based rewards, suggesting that test-time computation scaling naturally induces adaptive resource-rational behavior.

### 2025 — Computer Vision Modeling of the Development of Geometric and Numerical Concepts in Humans
*arXiv preprint (arXiv:2511.15029)*
Authors: Zekun Wang, Sashank Varma

This study evaluates the developmental alignment between computer vision (CV) architectures and the maturation of mathematical cognition in human children. Using the ResNet-50 architecture as a computational case study, the researchers analyze the progression of latent representations for geometric and numerical concepts across ImageNet training checkpoints. The results demonstrate developmental alignment for specific geometric and topological (GT) classes, such as Euclidean geometry and metric properties, where the model's performance improvements track the sensitivity trajectories observed in human children. Additionally, the research identifies the induction of a human-like mental number line (MNL), exhibiting characteristic distance and ratio effects, which suggests that abstract mathematical intuitions can be acquired through learning to categorize complex natural visual environments without specialized innate circuitry.

### 2025 — Modeling Understanding of Story-Based Analogies Using Large Language Models
*Proceedings of the 47th Annual Meeting of the Cognitive Science Society (CogSci 2025)*
Authors: Kalit Inani, Keshav Kabra, Vijay Marupudi, Sashank Varma

Building on the seminal analogical reasoning studies of Gentner and Holyoak, this research provides a fine-grained evaluation of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment with human performance on story-based mapping tasks. The study first assesses the semantic latent space of models by determining whether BERT-based sentence embeddings effectively differentiate between structural analogical targets and surface-similar distractors. Second, the authors evaluate the impact of enhanced two-step generative prompting strategies designed to simulate human-like relational induction. By correlating item-level model accuracy with human data across a benchmark of story analogy problems, the study quantifies the extent to which state-of-the-art architectures (e.g., GPT-4, Llama 3) replicate the cognitive profiles and reasoning biases inherent in human analogical detecting and mapping.

### 2025 — Alignment of CNN and human judgments of geometric and topological concepts
*Proceedings of the 39th AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI 2025)*
Authors: Neha Upadhyay, Vijay Marupudi, Kamala Varma, Sashank Varma

This study probes the cognitive alignment between deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and human sensitivity to geometric and topological (GT) concepts. Evaluated through a six-image odd-one-out task testing 43 concepts (e.g., topology, Euclidean geometry, symmetry), the performance of five architectures—including ResNet-18 and DenseNet—is compared against behavioral data from children and adults. The investigation assesses whether CNNs can serve as a sufficiency proof for the 'learning account,' which posits that mathematical concepts are induced from visual experience rather than supported by innate 'core knowledge' circuitry. The results show that while CNNs successfully model human sensitivity to classes like Euclidean geometry, they diverge significantly on geometric transformations, with specific architectures like DenseNet aligning most closely with the performance profiles of children aged 3–6 years.

### 2025 — Estimation of factorial expressions and its improvement through calibration: A replication and extension of Tversky and Kahneman (1973)
*Memory & Cognition*
Authors: Jeffrey K. Bye, Vijay Marupudi, Reba Koenen, Jimin Park, Sashank Varma

This research replicates and extends the classic psychological study by Tversky and Kahneman (1973) regarding the estimation of factorial products (e.g., 1x2x...x8 vs. 8x7x...x1). The study investigates the robustness of the anchoring and adjustment heuristic by varying the presentation direction of factorial sequences and introducing a feedback-driven calibration phase. Participants provided quantitative estimates of factorial products, and the researchers measured the resulting systematic underestimation and the efficacy of calibration in mitigating these biases. The findings quantify the persistence of anchoring effects in complex numerical judgments and demonstrate that targeted calibration significantly enhances the accuracy and precision of human intuitions for computationally intensive mathematical growth functions.
