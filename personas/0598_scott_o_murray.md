# Scott O Murray

*visual perception and attention* — University of Washington, Department of Psychology, Vision & Neuroimaging Research Laboratory.

## Background

Scott O Murray is a Professor of Psychology who researches the neural and computational mechanisms underlying human visual perception and attention. Utilizing high-resolution functional MRI (fMRI) and computational modeling, Murray characterizes how the visual cortex represents complex features such as shape, size, and lightness, exploring how contextual information and top-down attention modulate early sensory processing. A portion of their recent research focuses on neurodiversity, applying vision science frameworks to study altered cortical computations and excitation-inhibition balance in individuals with autism spectrum disorder. By linking large-scale brain activity patterns to perceptual behaviors, Murray’s laboratory aims to identify the principles of how the brain constructs a representation of the environment.

## Papers

### 2026 — Cognitively-Inspired Tokens Overcome Egocentric Bias in Multimodal Models
*arXiv*
Authors: Bridget Leonard, Scott O. Murray

Multimodal language models (MLMs) typically exhibit robust performance on semantic vision-language tasks but frequently fail in spatial reasoning scenarios that require the adoption of an external agent's visual perspective. The authors attribute this deficiency to a systemic egocentric bias inherent in propositional image representations. To address this, they propose 'perspective tokens'—specialized embeddings that integrate orientation information through either embodied body-keypoint cues or abstract mental rotation mechanisms. Implementing these tokens within a LLaVA-1.5-13B framework leads to significant accuracy improvements in level-2 visual perspective-taking (VPT) across several synthetic and naturalistic benchmarks, including Isle Bricks V2 and 3DSRBench, demonstrating that embedding analog-like spatial structures into the token space enhances allocentric reasoning.

### 2025 — Why Multimodal Models Struggle with Spatial Reasoning: Insights from Human Cognition
*Proceedings of the 47th Annual Meeting of the Cognitive Science Society (CogSci)*
Authors: Bridget Leonard, Kristin Woodard, Scott O. Murray

This study investigates the divergence between multimodal language models (MLMs) and human spatial cognition, focusing on the absence of dorsal-stream-like computations in artificial systems. While current models effectively fuse linguistic data with ventral-stream-like visual features, they lack the computational architecture necessary for visual-motor integration and mental rotation. Utilizing a visual perspective-taking benchmark derived from developmental psychology, the authors evaluate GPT-4o and find that its performance on tasks involving viewpoint shifts matches the developmental level of a two-year-old child. The findings suggest that existing MLMs rely on propositional representations that fail to support the analog transformations required for robust allocentric spatial reasoning.

### 2025 — Stimulus-Independent Modulation of Cerebellar Neural Activity by Visual Spatial Attention
*Journal of Vision*
Authors: Brenda (Siyue) Qiu, Kristin M. Woodard, Bridget Leonard, Scott O. Murray

The authors examine the role of the cerebellum in non-motor cognitive processes, specifically covert visual spatial attention, using high-field fMRI. By implementing a baseline-shift paradigm that isolates top-down attentional signals from stimulus-evoked responses, the study measures BOLD activity in cerebellar subregions during an orientation discrimination task. The results demonstrate that directed spatial attention modulates neural activity in the cerebellum independently of the physical properties or presence of the visual stimulus. This stimulus-independent activity suggests the cerebellum participates in maintaining top-down attentional priority maps, extending its functional repertoire beyond traditional motor control into the domain of visual cognitive regulation.

### 2024 — Failures in Perspective-taking of Multimodal AI Systems
*arXiv*
Authors: Bridget Leonard, Kristin Woodard, Scott O. Murray

Leveraging established methodologies from cognitive and developmental science, this research probes the spatial representation capabilities of state-of-the-art multimodal AI systems, specifically GPT-4o. The study distinguishes between Level 1 perspective-taking, which concerns object visibility, and Level 2, which requires determining how an object appears from a specific orientation. Experimental results indicate that while multimodal models exhibit competency in Level 1 tasks, they show a significant performance decrement in Level 2 tasks that necessitate mental rotation and spatial transformation. The authors conclude that spatial representations in current multimodal systems are predominantly propositional and lack the analog fidelity required to mirror human-like perspective-taking development.

### 2024 — Investigating the Relationship Between Alpha-Band Power and Subcortical Structure Volumes in Autism
*Biological Psychiatry*
Authors: Daniela L. Seczon, Mark W. Pettet, Hannah M. Rea, Scott O. Murray

Atypical alpha-band (~10 Hz) neural oscillations have been linked to the sensory hypersensitivity and altered gain control observed in individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). This study explores the neuroanatomical contributors to these oscillatory differences by combining EEG spectral analysis with structural MRI volumetry. The researchers quantified alpha amplitude across stimulus-driven and resting-state conditions and examined its correlation with subcortical volumes, including the thalamus and hippocampus. The findings reveal a significant diagnosis-by-hippocampal volume interaction, indicating that structural characteristics of subcortical regions may differentially modulate cortical alpha oscillations in autistic compared to neurotypical adults, providing a potential structural basis for atypical sensory behavior in ASD.
