# Sheng He

*visual attention and awareness* — Chinese Academy of Sciences, State Key Laboratory of Cognitive Science, Vision and Attention Lab.

## Background

Sheng He is a cognitive neuroscientist researching the functional and neural mechanisms of human vision, focusing on visual attention and the limits of conscious awareness. Using psychophysical methods and high-field (7T) functional MRI, they characterize how the brain processes visual information from early cortical stages to high-level representations, including responses to stimuli below the threshold of awareness. Their work examines binocular rivalry, the resolution of visual competition, and the role of feedback signals in the human visual cortex. They currently investigate the mesoscale neural circuitry of the visual system and the integration of visual features into coherent perception.

## Papers

### 2025 — The distinct role of human PIT in attention control
*eLife*
Authors: Siyuan Huang, Lan Wang, Sheng He

This study employs 7T high-resolution functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) to investigate the human posterior inferotemporal cortex (hPIT) as a candidate for a neural priority map. Utilizing spatial attention paradigms across multiple feature domains (motion, color, and shape), the research demonstrates that hPIT exhibits robust attentional modulation that is independent of stimulus category but highly sensitive to attentional demands. Functional connectivity analyses reveal that hPIT acts as an integrative hub bridging the dorsal and ventral attention networks, facilitating the orchestration of endogenous and exogenous control signals to optimize behavioral responses to complex visual environments.

### 2025 — Retrospective attention can trigger the conscious representation of invisible stimuli
*eLife (Reviewed Preprint)*
Authors: Lan Wang, Sheng He

This research utilizes a retrospective cueing paradigm combined with continuous flash suppression (CFS) to explore the recovery of visual information from stimuli that failed to reach awareness at the time of presentation. The study provides behavioral and neural evidence that a retro-cue delivered after the offset of a suppressed stimulus can retroactively facilitate its access into conscious awareness. These findings indicate the existence of a high-fidelity, pre-conscious buffer or latent representation that remains accessible to top-down attentional selection for a brief temporal window, suggesting that attention can boost past sensory events into phenomenal awareness.

### 2025 — Duration adaptation depends on the perceived rather than physical duration and can be observed across sensory modalities
*Perception*
Authors: Hao Chen, Sheng He

By investigating duration adaptation effects through a series of psychophysical experiments, this study examines whether temporal adaptation is driven by objective physical duration or subjective perceptual duration. The results show that duration aftereffects from visual adapters align more closely with subjectively matched durations than with their physical lengths. Furthermore, the study identifies a cross-modal adaptation effect where adapting to a visual stimulus influences the perceived duration of a subsequent auditory stimulus, provided the subjective durations are matched. These findings suggest that duration is encoded at a modality-independent, high-level processing stage susceptible to top-down perceptual biases.

### 2024 — Characterizing Frequency Response Functions of Low-Level and High-Level Stimuli in the Human Brain
*Journal of Vision*
Authors: Na Zhao, LiNan Shi, Sheng He

This investigation characterizes the frequency-specific neural responses and entrainment elicited by varying levels of visual complexity (low-level checkerboard patterns vs. high-level faces and landscapes) flickering at 20, 30, 40, and 48 Hz. Using 7T fMRI and Magnetoencephalography (MEG), the study demonstrates that high-level stimuli trigger significantly broader temporal lobe activation and more effective gamma-band entrainment in deep brain structures, including the hippocampus, compared to low-level stimuli. The results indicate that stimulus representational complexity significantly modulates the efficiency of rhythmic neural drive throughout the human cortical and subcortical hierarchy.

### 2024 — Brain-inspired artificial intelligence research: A review
*Science China Information Sciences*
Authors: Guoyin Wang, Huanan Bao, Qun Liu, Tiangang Zhou, Si Wu, Tiejun Huang, Zhaofei Yu, Cewu Lu, Yihong Gong, Zhaoxiang Zhang, Sheng He

Structured around David Marr’s three-layer framework (computational, algorithmic, and implementation), this comprehensive review analyzes the theoretical and functional intersections between cognitive neuroscience and artificial intelligence. The paper evaluates the divergence between biological cognition mechanisms and current machine learning architectures, identifying specific limitations in the realization of human-like intelligence in AI. It proposes eight strategic research directions, including bionic information processing, embodied intelligence, and collaborative perceptual-interpretive mechanisms, aimed at narrowing the structural and algorithmic gap between human brains and artificial systems.
