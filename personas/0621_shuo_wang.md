# Shuo Wang

*social attention and face processing* — Washington University in St. Louis, Department of Radiology, Shuo Wang Lab.

## Background

Shuo Wang is an Associate Professor of Radiology and Neurological Surgery who investigates the neural mechanisms of social attention, face processing, and decision-making. Wang’s research utilizes a multimodal approach, integrating human single-neuron recordings, eye tracking, fMRI, and computational modeling to examine how the brain processes socially relevant stimuli. Their work characterizes atypical neural encoding and visual saliency in individuals with autism spectrum disorder to facilitate clinical interventions.

## Papers

### 2026 — Computational neural dynamics of goal-directed visual attention in macaques
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Jie Zhang, Nuttida Rungratsameetaweemana, Shuo Wang

This study employs large-scale neural population recordings from cortical areas V4, IT, OFC, and LPFC in macaque monkeys performing a free-gaze visual search task with naturalistic stimuli. The researchers identify distinct neural subspaces specialized for encoding category-specific task goals and stimulus features. By examining dynamics within an orthogonal subspace of the neural state space, the authors demonstrate that foveal attention enhances peripheral representations through increased pattern separation and context-dependent geometric reshaping. The findings reveal that pre-search cue-related activity predicts subsequent search efficiency and that low-dimensional population trajectories integrate task demands with perceptual input to coordinate goal-directed attention.

### 2026 — Characterization of the spatiotemporal representations of visual, semantic, and memorability features in the human brain
*PLOS Biology*
Authors: Yue Wang, Peter Brunner, Jon T. Willie, Runnan Cao, Shuo Wang

Utilizing high-resolution intracranial electroencephalography (iEEG) from 5,143 channels, this research maps the spatiotemporal neural encoding of visual, semantic, and memorability features during object perception. The study demonstrates that while the ventral temporal cortex (VTC) processes all three attributes, they exhibit distinct temporal trajectories: visual features emerge first, followed by semantic and then memorability information. Cross-modal validation using 7T fMRI and single-neuron recordings from the medial temporal lobe (MTL) reveals that memorability is an intrinsic stimulus property encoded early in the visual hierarchy. The results further indicate that prefrontal cortex representations of memorability arise from the integration of VTC signals, suggesting an integrated computational pathway connecting perception and memory.

### 2025 — Gamma synchronization between the medial temporal lobe and medial frontal cortex for goal-directed visual attention in humans
*Cell Reports*
Authors: Jie Zhang, Jing Xia, Huihui Zhou, Shuo Wang

The study investigates the long-range coordination between the human medial temporal lobe (MTL) and medial frontal cortex (MFC) using simultaneous single-neuron and local field potential (LFP) recordings. During a goal-directed visual search, the researchers identified target-dependent gamma-band synchronization (30–80 Hz) as a primary mechanism for attentional control. Specifically, the amygdala shows enhanced spike-LFP phase-locking with the dACC during goal maintenance (working memory), while dACC spiking synchronizes with MTL LFPs during target detection and decision execution. These findings delineate a functional circuit where task-dependent oscillatory synchronization facilitates the alignment of representational geometry across frontal and temporal nodes to support active vision.

### 2025 — A neuronal code for object representation and memory in the human amygdala and hippocampus
*Nature Communications*
Authors: Runnan Cao, Peter Brunner, Puneeth N. Chakravarthula, Krista L. Wahlstrom, Cory Inman, Elliot H. Smith, Xin Li, Adam N. Mamelak, Nicholas J. Brandmeir, Ueli Rutishauser, Jon T. Willie, Shuo Wang

Through recordings of 3,173 single neurons in neurosurgical patients, this paper identifies a 'region-based feature code' for visual objects within the human amygdala and hippocampus. Unlike sparse category-selective coding, these neurons exhibit receptive fields in a high-dimensional semantic feature space, enabling the flexible representation of diverse stimuli. The study demonstrates that the strength of this region-based neural response during encoding predicts successful memory retrieval and parametrically tracks image memorability. These results provide a unified framework for how the medial temporal lobe (MTL) transforms high-level visual features into stable representations that serve as the building blocks for episodic and semantic memory.

### 2025 — A human single-neuron dataset for object recognition
*Scientific Data*
Authors: Runnan Cao, Peter Brunner, Nicholas J. Brandmeir, Jon T. Willie, Shuo Wang

This data descriptor provides a large-scale dataset of single-neuron activity recorded from the human amygdala and hippocampus of 15 neurosurgical patients. The resource includes spike-sorted unit activity and synchronized intracranial LFP data from 1,716 neurons across multiple experiments using ImageNet and Microsoft COCO stimuli. The dataset captures neural responses during passive viewing and memory tasks, providing unprecedented spatial and temporal resolution for investigating category selectivity, sparse coding, and representational transformations in the medial temporal lobe. The released data, including electrode localization in MNI space and behavioral metrics, is intended to facilitate the development and validation of biologically constrained computational models of human object recognition.
