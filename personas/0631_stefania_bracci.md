# Stefania Bracci

*visual object representation* — University of Trento, CIMeC - Center for Mind/Brain Sciences, Object Vision Group.

## Background

Stefania Bracci is an Associate Professor at the University of Trento and a researcher within the Object Vision Group at the Center for Mind/Brain Sciences (CIMeC). Bracci’s research investigates the organizational principles of object representations in the human visual cortex, examining the interplay between visual features, conceptual categories, and action-related properties. Utilizing functional MRI, representational similarity analysis, and computational modeling with deep neural networks, they study the topographic mapping of tool and hand representations in the occipitotemporal cortex. Their work further explores how task context and experience shape neural representational spaces, comparing biological visual processing with artificial intelligence models.

## Papers

### 2025 — Investigating action topography in visual cortex and deep artificial neural networks
*Nature Communications*
Authors: Davide Cortinovis, Nhut Truong, Hans Op de Beeck, Stefania Bracci

The topographic architecture of high-order visual cortex is characterized by category-selective regions organized within large-scale maps such as real-world size and animacy. This study identifies action as a primary organizing principle of object space in the human visual system. Utilizing fMRI and a stimulus set of body parts and objects with varying action properties, we identified a topographically organized action gradient in the left lateral occipitotemporal cortex (LOTC). This gradient transitions from dorsal-posterior to ventral-anterior, showing selective and partially overlapping activations for bodies, hands, tools, and manipulable objects, converging at the representation of categories with high action relevance (hands and tools). Multivariate pattern analyses demonstrated that action properties better explain LOTC representations than shape or animacy. Conversely, ventral occipitotemporal cortex (VOTC) and topographic deep artificial neural networks (TDANNs) were dominated by animacy and shape information, with TDANNs failing to replicate the action-based organization observed in the human brain. These results suggest that the LOTC encodes behaviorally relevant action dimensions that are not captured by standard object recognition models.

### 2025 — Tool Representations in Human Visual Cortex
*Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience*
Authors: Davide Cortinovis, Marius V. Peelen, Stefania Bracci

This review synthesizes decades of neuroimaging research to characterize the representational spaces in the visual ventral and dorsal streams specifically for tools and action-related objects. The evidence reveals a functional dissociation between lateral and ventral occipitotemporal cortex (OTC). Lateral OTC represents both the visual shape and action-related dimensions of objects, effectively grouping objects that serve as end-effectors (e.g., hands and tools) separately from other manipulable objects. In contrast, ventral OTC primarily encodes visual properties such as surface material, texture, and real-world size, reflecting a more general object-feature code. The paper evaluates the capacity of artificial neural networks to model these action-related dimensions, noting that while deep learning models approximate category-selectivity, they often fail to capture the behaviorally relevant action codes that distinguish tools in the human brain. We propose a dimensional framework where OTC regions support tool representations through a combination of visual features and action-relevant information, suggesting a path forward for biologically plausible computational models of object space.
