# Uri Hasson

*cognitive neuroscience and neuroAI* — University of Trento, Center for Mind/Brain Sciences (CIMeC), Information and Integration Lab.

## Background

Uri Hasson is an Associate Professor at the University of Trento and the Principal Investigator of the Information and Integration Lab at the Center for Mind/Brain Sciences (CIMeC). Hasson’s research investigates the neurobiological principles underlying language comprehension, the processing of statistical regularities, and how the brain manages uncertainty in both visual and auditory domains. Recently, their work has expanded into the field of neuroAI, focusing on the alignment between human cognitive representations and artificial neural networks, such as deep vision models and large language models. Hasson utilizes fMRI, EEG, and computational modeling to explore how the brain integrates complex information and generates predictions in real-world contexts.

## Papers

### 2026 — Beyond topography: Topographic regularization improves robustness and reshapes representations in convolutional neural networks
*Neurocomputing*
Authors: Nhut Truong, Uri Hasson

This research investigates the influence of topographic constraints on the representational geometry and functional robustness of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). By imposing local spatial regularization on a penultimate-layer topographic grid, the authors demonstrate that such constraints significantly enhance the network's resilience to internal architecture and weight perturbations. The topographic regularization systematically reconfigures the latent feature space into a more brain-aligned spatial layout, suggesting that spatial organization is a critical inductive bias for developing robust, biologically-plausible artificial vision systems.

### 2025 — Modeling Human Concepts with Subspaces in Deep Vision Models
*ACM Transactions on Interactive Intelligent Systems*
Authors: Anna Bavaresco, Nhut Truong, Uri Hasson

This study addresses the representational divergence between artificial vision models and human conceptual structures by identifying category-specific subspaces within Deep Neural Networks (DNNs). Using a supervised feature-pruning approach optimized to align with human similarity judgments for specific semantic domains (e.g., animals, food), the authors demonstrate that different categories utilize distinct, non-overlapping latent feature sets. These category-specific subspaces capture relevant human-like variance and provide a computational metric for evaluating the conceptual alignment and fidelity of AI-generated imagery.

### 2025 — Quantifying sighting dominance using on-display projections of monocular and binocular views
*Behavior Research Methods*
Authors: Giuseppe Notaro, Uri Hasson

The authors propose a high-precision, quantitative methodology for the assessment of sighting ocular dominance, grounded in a two-camera geometric model. The method utilizes a binocular alignment task on a digital display to identify the relative weighting of each eye's contribution to the fused percept by comparing perceived target projections under monocular and binocular viewing. The resulting continuous index demonstrates exceptional test-retest reliability (ICC = 0.96) and is influenced by physiological parameters such as interpupillary distance and phoria, revealing systematic nearly-balanced vision in specific individuals.

### 2025 — Explaining Human Comparisons Using Alignment-Importance Heatmaps
*Computational Brain & Behavior*
Authors: Nhut Truong, Dario Pesenti, Uri Hasson

This paper introduces the Alignment Importance Score (AIS) to quantify the contribution of individual DNN feature maps to the representational alignment with human similarity judgments. The researchers generate image-specific heatmaps that identify diagnostic visual regions for comparative tasks. While these AIS-based heatmaps largely correspond to traditional gaze-prediction saliency, they successfully identify non-salient features that are nonetheless critical for conceptual comparisons, providing a more granular understanding of human-like decision-making in vision models.

### 2024 — Neural networks associated with eye movements in congenital blindness
*European Journal of Neuroscience*
Authors: Cemal Koba, Alessandro Crimi, Olivier Collignon, Emiliano Ricciardi, Uri Hasson

This resting-state fMRI study investigates the preservation and reorganization of oculomotor-related neural circuitry in the absence of visual input. By extracting orbital echo-planar imaging (EPI) signals as a regressor, the authors demonstrate that congenitally blind individuals retain functional connectivity between eye-orbit signals and the motor cortex, although coupling with the visual cortex is significantly attenuated compared to sighted controls. The results reveal a moderate large-scale reorganization of functional networks and instances of interocular decoherence, detailing the impact of long-term sensory deprivation on the endogenous control of eye movements.
