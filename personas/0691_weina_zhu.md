# Weina Zhu

*visual awareness and consciousness* — Yunnan University, School of Information Science.

## Background

Weina Zhu is a Professor in the School of Information Science at Yunnan University, specializing in the cognitive neuroscience of visual perception. Zhu’s research investigates how the human brain processes sensory information within and outside of conscious awareness, utilizing continuous flash suppression (CFS) paradigms and psychophysical modeling. They employ methodologies including high-density electroencephalography (EEG/ERP), eye-tracking, and image processing to examine the temporal dynamics of vision, the 'closure-superiority effect,' and the role of neural oscillations in defining temporal integration windows. This work connects computational mathematics and vision science to characterize mechanisms underlying rapid object recognition and visual attention.

## Papers

### 2025 — FA-STTM: A hybrid Transformer-Mamba network for motor imagery EEG classification
*Neurocomputing*
Authors: Mingjie Gao, Caicai Gao, Zifeng Yao, Weina Zhu

The authors present FA-STTM, a novel hybrid architecture that synthesizes the global dependency modeling of Transformers with the linear-time complexity of Mamba-based state-space models (SSMs) to optimize motor imagery (MI) electroencephalogram (EEG) decoding. The framework utilizes a Transformer encoder to capture broad spatiotemporal correlations across EEG channels and temporal segments, while a Mamba-based decoder effectively handles long-range sequence modeling with significantly reduced computational overhead compared to quadratic attention mechanisms. Validated on the BCI Competition IV-2a and IV-2b datasets, the model demonstrates robust classification performance against signal non-stationarity and inter-subject variability, outperforming established convolutional benchmarks in cross-subject generalization and inference efficiency.

### 2025 — EMSA: Explainable multilingual sentiment analysis models providing sentiment analysis across multiple languages
*PLOS ONE*
Authors: Li Zhao, Jinwei Zhou, Jinde Cao, Weina Zhu

This study introduces the Explainable Multilingual Sentiment Analyzer (EMSA), a novel natural language processing framework designed to enhance the interpretability of cross-lingual sentiment classification. Leveraging large language models (LLMs) and chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, EMSA bifurcates the sentiment analysis task into an explicit reasoning phase and a final categorization phase. The reasoning module generates intermediate linguistic and contextual justifications, providing transparent logic for the subsequent sentiment label (positive, negative, or neutral). Evaluation on the Chinese financial GubaSenti dataset and the English SST benchmark indicates that EMSA achieves superior performance over traditional transformer-based encoders like RoBERTa and XLNet, particularly in handling domain-specific nuances and linguistic diversity while increasing user trust through verifiable explainability.

### 2025 — MIFNet: a Mamba-based interactive frequency convolutional neural network for motor imagery decoding
*Cognitive Neurodynamics*
Authors: Luoqian Yang, Weina Zhu

To address the challenges of low signal-to-noise ratio and complex spatiotemporal dynamics in EEG-based motor imagery (MI), the researchers developed MIFNet, an interactive frequency convolutional neural network incorporating a Mamba temporal module. The architecture utilizes a non-overlapping frequency decomposition strategy to isolate task-relevant mu (8–12 Hz) and beta (12–32 Hz) rhythms. A specialized ConvEncoder fuses spectral-spatial features from these decomposed bands, while a selective state-space model (SSM) based on the Mamba architecture captures long-range temporal dependencies with linear complexity. Experimental results across BCIC-IV-2A and High-Gamma datasets show that MIFNet significantly enhances decoding accuracy and robustness by bridging the gap between localized feature extraction and global temporal modeling.

### 2025 — The role of scene context in object recognition: Timing and mechanisms
*Journal of Vision*
Authors: Mingjie Gao, Jan Drewes, Weina Zhu

This psychophysical investigation explores how environmental consistency modulates the temporal dynamics and underlying mechanisms of visual object identification. Utilizing a dual-paradigm approach involving plain 2-alternative forced choice (2AFC) and binocular continuous flash suppression (CFS), the study examines the 'scene consistency effect' under conscious and unconscious viewing conditions. Results indicate that upright, congruent backgrounds facilitate target categorization primarily in the early stages of visual processing. The findings reveal a category-specific sensitivity, where furniture targets demonstrate a more pronounced reliance on contextual regularities for identification compared to animal stimuli, suggesting that high-level semantic schemas and global scene gist selectively modulate lower-level feature integration and breakthrough times from suppression.

### 2025 — TMSA-Net: A novel attention mechanism for improved motor imagery EEG signal processing
*Biomedical Signal Processing and Control*
Authors: Qian Zhao, Weina Zhu

The authors propose TMSA-Net, an end-to-end deep learning framework featuring a Temporal-Multi-Scale-Attention (TMSA) module specifically engineered for motor imagery (MI) EEG decoding. The architecture addresses the inherent non-stationarity and subject-specific variability of brain signals by adaptively assigning importance weights to specific channel-wise frequency bands and temporal windows. By integrating a multi-scale convolutional backbone with this selective attention mechanism, the model enhances the discriminability of latent neural patterns associated with motor intent. Comparative evaluations on the BCI Competition IV dataset 2a, 2b and the OpenBMI dataset demonstrate that TMSA-Net achieves statistically significant improvements in kappa values and mean classification accuracy relative to standard EEGNet and DeepConvNet architectures.
