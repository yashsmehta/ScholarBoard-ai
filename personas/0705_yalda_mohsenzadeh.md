# Yalda Mohsenzadeh

*cognitive computational neuroscience* — Western University, Department of Computer Science, Cognitive Neuroscience & Artificial Intelligence Lab.

## Background

Mohsenzadeh is an Assistant Professor at Western University and a faculty member at the Vector Institute, focusing on the intersection of human visual cognition and artificial intelligence. Their research employs multi-modal neuroimaging techniques, including fMRI, MEG, and EEG, alongside deep learning architectures to decode the spatiotemporal dynamics of the human brain during visual processing and memory formation. By examining the computational principles of biological vision, such as image memorability and robust perception, Mohsenzadeh aims to develop machines that emulate human-level performance and resilience.

## Papers

### 2025 — Efficient slice anomaly detection network for 3D brain MRI Volume
*PLOS Digital Health*
Authors: Zeduo Zhang, Yalda Mohsenzadeh

This research introduces a computationally efficient anomaly detection framework for 3D brain MRI volumes that mitigates the memory-intensive requirements of traditional 3D convolutional neural networks. The proposed architecture employs a slice-based approach utilizing a triplet loss training objective to characterize the manifold of normal anatomical variability. By operating on 2D slices while preserving inter-slice spatial consistency, the model identifies pathological deviations as anomalies. Evaluation on T1 and T2-weighted brain volumes demonstrates that this methodology surpasses existing reconstruction-based models in detecting spatial anomalies, offering significant improvements in inference speed and reducing the necessity for complex post-processing of noisy reconstruction outputs.

### 2025 — Modeling Visual Memorability Assessment with Autoencoders Reveals Characteristics of Memorable Images
*arXiv (cs.CV)*
Authors: Elham Bagheri, Yalda Mohsenzadeh

This study investigates the computational underpinnings of visual memorability using an unsupervised deep learning framework. The authors propose an autoencoder-based model, utilizing a VGG16 backbone, trained in a single-epoch regime to approximate human memory recall after a single exposure. The analysis reveals a significant positive correlation between the autoencoder's reconstruction error and human memorability scores, indicating that highly memorable images occupy distinct regions of the visual feature space that challenge standard compression algorithms. Furthermore, the distinctiveness of the latent representations was found to be a robust predictor of memorability. Interpretability analysis via Integrated Gradients (IG) identified specific visual characteristics that contribute to an image's intrinsic memorability within the model's latent space.

### 2025 — Neural processing of naturalistic audiovisual events in space and time
*Communications Biology*
Authors: Yu Hu, Yalda Mohsenzadeh

Using a combination of fMRI and MEG data fusion alongside deep neural network (DNN) modeling, this study maps the spatiotemporal dynamics of multisensory integration for naturalistic audiovisual stimuli. The researchers utilized GIST descriptors for visual features, Cochleagram models for acoustic features, and GloVe word embeddings for semantic content to perform representational similarity analysis (RSA). The results demonstrate asymmetrical neural representations of early cross-modal interactions, with acoustic features exhibiting significant correlations in both auditory and early ventral visual regions. Searchlight analysis confirmed a hierarchical progression where early DNN layers align with primary sensory cortices and deeper layers correspond to higher-level associative regions, providing a comprehensive characterization of how naturalistic multisensory events are synthesized in the human brain.

### 2025 — Multivariate Pattern Analysis of EEG Reveals Neural Mechanism of Naturalistic Target Processing in Attentional Blink
*Journal of Neuroscience*
Authors: Mansoure Jahanian, Marc F. Joanisse, Boyu Wang, Yalda Mohsenzadeh

This paper investigates the neural mechanisms of the attentional blink (AB) phenomenon using high-temporal resolution EEG and multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA). Participants performed a rapid serial visual presentation (RSVP) task involving naturalistic target images. Decoding of the neural signals revealed that the 'blink' (failure to perceive the second target, T2) is primarily driven by a disruption in late-stage neural representations (200–500 ms post-stimulus) rather than a failure of initial sensory encoding. The study finds that while naturalistic stimuli evoke more robust and persistent neural signatures than simplified targets, they are still subject to the capacity-limited bottleneck of conscious processing. These findings clarify the temporal window where attentional resources are depleted during the processing of complex, real-world visual information.

### 2024 — Unveiling the neural dynamics of conscious perception in rapid object recognition
*NeuroImage*
Authors: Saba Charmi Motlagh, Marc Joanisse, Boyu Wang, Yalda Mohsenzadeh

To delineate the temporal evolution of conscious object perception, this study integrates EEG recordings with representational similarity analysis (RSA) during a rapid visual presentation task. The researchers utilized a sliding-window decoding approach to track the emergence and maintenance of category-level neural information under varying levels of stimulus visibility. The findings highlight a hierarchical progression: initial low-level feature encoding occurs within 100 ms, followed by category-specific representations between 150 and 250 ms. The transition to conscious perception was uniquely associated with the sustained stability and recurrence of these neural representations in later processing stages, whereas non-consciously perceived stimuli were characterized by a rapid decay of categorical information.
