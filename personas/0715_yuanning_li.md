# Yuanning Li

*computational and cognitive neuroscience* — ShanghaiTech University, School of Biomedical Engineering, Computational Cognitive and Translational Neuroscience Lab (CCTN Lab).

## Background

Yuanning Li is an Assistant Professor at ShanghaiTech University who researches the neural mechanisms of high-level cognitive processes, including speech, language, and vision. Li's work uses intracranial electrophysiology (ECoG/iEEG) and functional neuroimaging to investigate how sensory and semantic information is represented across neural populations. By integrating statistical machine learning and deep neural networks, they develop computational models for neural encoding and decoding to support brain-computer interface (BCI) applications. Li completed a postdoctoral fellowship at UCSF focused on speech neurophysiology and a doctoral dissertation at Carnegie Mellon University concerning the human ventral visual pathway.

## Papers

### 2026 — ITD-aware Binaural Spiking Networks for Sound Source Localization
*IEEE International Conference on Acoustics, Speech and Signal Processing (ICASSP 2026)*
Authors: Nanjun Ye, Li Fu, Yuanning Li

The paper proposes a biologically inspired spiking neural network (SNN) architecture tailored for sound source localization (SSL) by specifically leveraging interaural time difference (ITD) cues, mimicking the processing mechanisms found in the mammalian brainstem. The model employs leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons and local synaptic learning rules to extract azimuthal features from binaural audio. Evaluations in reverberant and high-noise environments demonstrate that the ITD-aware SNN achieves robust localization accuracy with significantly lower computational and power overhead compared to conventional deep neural network (DNN) architectures. This work underscores the efficacy of temporal spike dynamics in reproducing specialized auditory perception for neuromorphic applications.

### 2025 — High-Fidelity Neural Speech Reconstruction through an Efficient Acoustic-Linguistic Dual-Pathway Framework
*eLife*
Authors: Jiawei Li, Chunxu Guo, Chao Zhang, Edward F. Chang, Yuanning Li

This research presents a high-performance brain-computer interface (BCI) system for decoding naturalistic speech from human intracranial electrocorticography (ECoG). The authors developed a dual-pathway framework that concurrently models acoustic prosody and linguistic intelligibility by aligning cortical features with the latent spaces of pre-trained self-supervised models (e.g., Wav2Vec 2.0) and high-fidelity generative decoders (e.g., HiFi-GAN and Parler-TTS). This transfer learning approach allows for high-fidelity speech synthesis using limited subject-specific neural recordings (~20 minutes). Perceptual and objective metrics indicate significant improvements in phoneme error rates and speech naturalness, demonstrating the potential for restoring intelligible communication with personalized voice characteristics.

### 2025 — Predictive vision-language integration in the human visual cortex
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Shurui Li, Zheyu Jin, Ru-Yuan Zhang, Shi Gu, Yuanning Li

This study investigates the neural substrates of cross-modal semantic integration using human fMRI and intracranial electrophysiology. By applying deep learning-based encoding models (LLMs and Vision Transformers), the authors demonstrate that linguistic context modulates neural response patterns in the early visual cortex (EVC). The study identifies distinct neuronal populations in the ventral stream that undergo semantic-driven enhancement or suppression, consistent with a predictive coding framework where linguistic priors act as top-down signals to shape visual representations. Spatiotemporal analysis reveals that these cross-modal semantic interactions occur early in the processing hierarchy, suggesting that the visual cortex is dynamically reweighted by linguistic information during naturalistic perception.

### 2025 — Optimizing Tonal Speech Decoding from Electrocorticography with a Multi-band Ensemble Network
*12th Annual IEEE International Conference on Neural Engineering (NER 25)*
Authors: Peixi Yu, Youran Mu, Zhenjie Wang, Daohan Zhang, Junfeng Lu, Yuanning Li

Decoding tonal languages like Mandarin for BCIs is complex because lexical meaning depends on fundamental frequency (F0) contours. This study introduces an ensemble neural network architecture designed to integrate features across multiple frequency bands of the electrocorticogram (ECoG), including both high-gamma power and lower-frequency spectral components. By optimizing the fusion of these multi-band features, the framework captures both the articulatory phonetics and the laryngeal dynamics associated with tone. Experimental results show a marked increase in syllable and lexical tone decoding accuracy compared to traditional single-band models, establishing a more effective approach for Mandarin-specific neural-to-speech decoding systems.

### 2024 — Convergent representations and spatiotemporal dynamics of speech and language in brain and deep neural networks
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Peili Chen, Shiji Xiang, Linyang He, Edward F. Chang, Yuanning Li

This paper quantifies the representational alignment between self-supervised deep neural networks (DNNs) and the human language network during natural speech perception. Utilizing high-density intracranial recordings (ECoG), the authors assessed the similarity between neural activity and the internal representations of speech (Wav2Vec 2.0) and text (GPT-2) models. They found that shared contextual components between the two modalities explain the majority of brain-model alignment in high-order temporal and prefrontal areas. The study further identified distinct spatiotemporal peaks: an early predictive signal (40ms pre-onset) in the mid-STG and a later acoustic-phonetic peak (200ms post-onset) in the posterior STG, providing a hierarchical map of how artificial and biological systems convergently process linguistic information.
