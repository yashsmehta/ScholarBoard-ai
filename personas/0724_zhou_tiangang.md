# Zhou Tiangang

*visual perception and topological structure* — Chinese Academy of Sciences, State Key Laboratory of Brain and Cognitive Science.

## Background

Zhou is a researcher at the State Key Laboratory of Brain and Cognitive Science, Institute of Biophysics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, where they investigate the neural mechanisms of visual cognition. Their work centers on the "global-first" theory of topological perception, which posits that the human visual system extracts global topological properties, such as the number of holes, as the primary units of object representation before processing local features. Using psychophysics, functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), and transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), Zhou examines the subcortical and cortical pathways involved in visual attention, object-based working memory, and the development of brain-inspired neural networks. Their research includes applying cognitive models to artificial intelligence and diagnosing mental disorders through dual-pathway fusion perceptual networks.

## Papers

### 2025 — Unravelling the object-based nature of visual working memory: insight from pointers
*Memory & Cognition*
Authors: Ning Wei, Jintao Song, Hongyi Zhang, Tiangang Zhou

This research investigates the representational units of visual working memory (VWM) by evaluating the efficacy of the pointer system in maintaining object correspondence during information updates. Utilizing a repetition benefit paradigm across three psychophysical experiments, we manipulated spatial coordinates, chromaticity, and morphological features as candidate pointer cues. The results indicate that while spatial indexing provides a robust primary cue for the pointer system, distinct feature-based information (shape and color) can effectively reestablish object-to-pointer mappings in VWM. These findings corroborate an object-based flexible pointer model, suggesting that the VWM system integrates multi-featural indices to preserve continuity across successive sensory inputs, rather than relying exclusively on spatial placeholders.

### 2024 — Bimodality Approach to Mental Disorder Diagnosis via Dual-pathway Fusion Perceptual Network
*2024 IEEE International Conference on Bioinformatics and Biomedicine (BIBM)*
Authors: Ruipeng Xu, Shuoqiu Gan, Feng Zhao, Zhentao Zuo, Tiangang Zhou

To enhance the diagnostic classification of psychiatric conditions such as schizophrenia and autism spectrum disorder, this study proposes a dual-pathway fusion perceptual (DP-FP) network for multimodal neuroimaging analysis. The architecture incorporates a coarse perception module that derives weighted feature representations from functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) functional connectivity data using cosine similarity metrics. By employing a multi-stage attentional perception mechanism, the model effectively integrates complementary information across data modalities. Experimental validation on the COBRE and ABIDE datasets demonstrated superior performance, achieving 87.6% accuracy for schizophrenia detection. Gradient-based interpretive analysis was further utilized to localize discriminative brain regions contributing to the classification results.

### 2024 — Brain Cognition-Inspired Dual-Pathway CNN Architecture for Image Classification
*IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems*
Authors: Songlin Dong, Chengli Tan, Zhentao Zuo, Yuhang He, Yihong Gong, Tiangang Zhou

Mimicking the human visual system's global-to-local processing hierarchy, this paper introduces a dual-pathway convolutional neural network (CNN) designed to improve image classification and robustness. The architecture bifurcates visual processing into two parallel streams: an 'analogy' pathway modeled after the subcortical retinotectal system for extracting coarse-grained topological invariants and shape-based gist, and a 'detail' pathway emulating the cortical ventral stream for capturing fine-grained textural and edge features. A top-down modulation framework facilitates the integration of these distinct feature sets. The resulting 'CogNet' demonstrates significantly enhanced out-of-distribution generalization and improved resilience against adversarial attacks on standard computer vision benchmarks compared to monolithic CNN architectures.

### 2024 — KDI-Net: dual-domain mapping network for undersampled magnetic resonance imaging reconstruction
*2024 International Conference on Image, Signal Processing, and Pattern Recognition (ISPP)*
Authors: Haiyang Guo, Zhentao Zuo, Dengdi Sun, Tiangang Zhou

KDI-Net is introduced as a novel hybrid-domain deep learning framework for reconstructing high-fidelity images from undersampled k-space magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) data. The network architecture consists of three interconnected modules—K-Block, D-Block, and I-Block—which perform concurrent operations in the frequency and image domains to preserve data consistency. By leveraging the underlying sparsity of k-space signals through cross-domain mapping, the model effectively suppresses aliasing artifacts and restores fine structural details. Quantitative evaluation using PSNR and SSIM metrics on the AUTOMAP and Calgary Campinas datasets revealed that KDI-Net consistently outperforms single-domain reconstruction methods, providing over 8% improvement in accuracy and facilitating 2-fold acceleration in data acquisition.

### 2024 — CMG: Class and mask guided network for hippocampus and its subregions segmentation
*2024 International Conference on Image, Signal Processing, and Pattern Recognition (ISPP)*
Authors: Zimu Wang, Zhentao Zuo, Dengdi Sun, Tiangang Zhou

This study addresses the anatomical complexity and low-contrast challenges of hippocampal subregion segmentation in MRI by proposing a Class and Mask Guided (CMG) network. The model utilizes a dual-guidance strategy: a semantic branch ensures categorical consistency across axial slices, while a mask-guidance branch imposes spatial boundary constraints to minimize leakage into adjacent structures. To capture the long-range dependencies necessary for delineating fine-grained subregional boundaries, the CMG network incorporates a hybrid attention mechanism within its encoder-decoder structure. Benchmarking results indicate that the dual-guidance approach provides significant improvements in Dice similarity coefficients for automatic segmentation tasks compared to standard U-Net and transformer-based baselines.
