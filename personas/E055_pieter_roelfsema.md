# Pieter Roelfsema

*visual perception and neuroprosthetics* — Netherlands Institute for Neuroscience, Vision and Cognition, Vision and Cognition Group.

## Background

Pieter Roelfsema is a neuroscientist specializing in the cortical mechanisms of visual perception, attention, and conscious experience. Their research utilizes primate electrophysiology, human neuroimaging, and computational modeling to investigate the function of the early visual cortex as a 'cognitive blackboard' during tasks such as perceptual grouping. A focus of their current work involves developing high-channel-count cortical neuroprostheses intended to restore rudimentary vision in blind individuals via electrical stimulation. Roelfsema's research group examines how feedback and lateral connections modulate neuronal activity to represent task-relevant features and facilitate object-background segregation.

## Papers

### 2025 — An extensive dataset of spiking activity to reveal the syntax of the ventral stream
*Neuron*
Authors: Paolo Papale, Feng Wang, Matthew W. Self, Pieter R. Roelfsema

The authors introduce the THINGS Ventral-stream Spiking Dataset (TVSD), a large-scale neurophysiological resource documenting the spiking activity of neuronal populations in macaque V1, V4, and inferotemporal (IT) cortex during the presentation of 25,000+ naturalistic images from the THINGS database. Utilizing high-channel-count electrode arrays, the study investigates the representational syntax of the ventral pathway, demonstrating that noise correlations scale with the similarity of neuronal tuning both within and across hierarchical levels. The dataset enables high-fidelity visualization of feature-tuning using deep convolutional neural networks and establishes a benchmark for evaluating the alignment between biological visual processing and computational vision models, providing a high-resolution map of object vision in the primate brain.

### 2025 — Large-scale mapping of artificial perceptions for neuroprostheses using spontaneous neuronal activity in macaque and human visual cortex
*Brain Stimulation*
Authors: Antonio Lozano, Xing Chen, Mike La Grouw, Beiyan Li, Feng Wang, Maureen van der Grinten, Cristian Soto-Sánchez, Alper Morales-Gregorio, Eduardo Fernández, Pieter R. Roelfsema

To address the clinical bottleneck of mapping phosphene locations for high-density visual prostheses, this research presents NEUmap, a framework for topography reconstruction using spontaneous neuronal activity. By applying Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) to correlations in multi-unit activity (MUA) and local field potentials (LFP) recorded during resting-state, the authors demonstrated that the intrinsic functional connectivity of the visual cortex preserves its retinotopic structure. The methodology was validated in sighted macaques and successfully translated to blind human volunteers with cortical implants, providing a rapid, calibration-free technique for aligning electrode coordinates with the visual field without requiring external visual input.

### 2025 — Modulatory feedback determines attentional object segmentation in a model of the ventral stream
*PLoS ONE*
Authors: Paolo Papale, Jonathan R. Williford, Stijn Balk, Pieter R. Roelfsema

This work examines how modulatory feedback signals implement attentional object segmentation within a recurrent model of the primate ventral stream. Integrating top-down modulatory connections that interact with feedforward feature processing, the model replicates physiological signatures of figure-ground modulation (FGM) and perceptual grouping. The authors show that these feedback mechanisms allow the network to selectively enhance object-consistent representations in early visual layers, facilitating robust segmentation in cluttered environments. Quantitative comparisons with macaque neurophysiology suggest that modulatory feedback is a critical computational primitive for solving the binding problem and directing object-based attention.

### 2024 — Pronouns reactivate conceptual representations in human hippocampal neurons
*Science*
Authors: Doris E. Dijksterhuis, Matthew W. Self, Jessy K. Possel, Johannes C. Peters, Erika C. W. van Straaten, Sander Idema, J. Christiaan Baaijen, Sandra M. A. van der Salm, Erik J. Aarnoutse, Nicole C. E. van Klink, Pieter van Eijsden, Simon Hanslmayr, Richard Chelvarajah, Florian Roux, Luca D. Kolibius, Victoria Sawlani, David T. Rollings, Stanislas Dehaene, Pieter R. Roelfsema

Utilizing intracranial single-unit recordings in the human hippocampus, the authors investigate the neural basis of pronoun-noun resolution during discourse comprehension. They identify a population of 'concept cells' that respond selectively to specific semantic identities (nouns) and demonstrate that these neurons are reactivated when subsequent pronouns refer back to those identities. This reactivation occurs rapidly and dynamically, supporting a model where semantic memory networks in the medial temporal lobe maintain conceptual continuity during language processing. The findings provide direct evidence for the integration of linguistic markers and stable conceptual representations at the single-neuron level.

### 2024 — Comparison of electrical microstimulation artifact removal methods for high-channel-count prostheses
*Journal of Neuroscience Methods*
Authors: Feng Wang, Xing Chen, Pieter R. Roelfsema

This technical study evaluates signal processing strategies for mitigating electrical microstimulation artifacts in high-channel-count cortical interfaces. The authors benchmarked local curve fitting (LCF), principal component analysis (PCA), and independent component analysis (ICA) on datasets recorded from multi-electrode arrays in macaques. Performance was assessed based on the fidelity of multi-unit activity (MUA) recovery and artifact suppression efficiency. Results demonstrated that PCA-based subtraction effectively handles the high spatial and temporal variability of artifacts across channels, enabling the extraction of clean neuronal responses during ongoing stimulation—a prerequisite for the development of adaptive, closed-loop visual neuroprostheses.
