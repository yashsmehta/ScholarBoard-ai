# Caspar Schwiedrzik

*cognitive neurobiology of learning and perception* — Ruhr University Bochum, Faculty of Biology and Biotechnology, Neural Circuits and Cognition Lab.

## Background

Caspar M. Schwiedrzik is a Professor of Cognitive Neurobiology at Ruhr University Bochum, where they investigate the neural mechanisms underlying learning and cognitive flexibility. Schwiedrzik utilizes a comparative approach that integrates electrophysiological recordings and functional neuroimaging in both humans and non-human primates to examine the interplay of feedforward and feedback signals within the visual hierarchy. Their research explores how the brain generates sensory predictions and how these top-down signals facilitate the learning of abstract models and identity recognition. By combining empirical data with computational modeling, Schwiedrzik seeks to identify the principles that allow cortical circuits to adapt to environmental changes and generalize across novel situations.

## Papers

### 2025 — Multiple task-demands flexibly optimize neural geometry in human ventral temporal cortex
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Tarana Nigam, Andrea F. Campos-Pérez, Pierre Mégevand, Juan R. Vidal, Marcela Perrone-Bertolotti, Philippe Kahane, Thomas Thesen, Orrin Devinsky, Lucia Melloni, Caspar M. Schwiedrzik

Utilizing intracranial electrophysiology in patients undergoing epilepsy monitoring, this study examined the neural underpinnings of behavioral flexibility during trial-by-trial task switching. Subjects performed three distinct tasks—individuation, categorization, and conceptualization—each requiring unique representational geometries for the same set of visual stimuli. Results indicated that neural representations do not undergo an immediate, cue-driven transformation; rather, task-specific geometries emerged incrementally over successive stimulus repetitions within a trial. The ventral temporal cortex was identified as the exclusive region exhibiting dynamic optimization of representational distances for all three tasks, with these gradual geometric refinements significantly predicting trial-level behavioral performance. These findings suggest that sensory cortices implement context-dependent optimization far earlier in the processing hierarchy than previously hypothesized.

### 2025 — Re-emergence of orientation coding in primate IT cortex and deep networks reveals functional hubs for visual processing
*bioRxiv*
Authors: Behnam Karami, Tarana Nigam, Sandrin S. Plewe, Caspar M. Schwiedrzik

Contrary to the efficient neural coding hypothesis which suggests that low-level features are progressively discarded in higher cortical areas, this study quantified orientation representation across the ventral stream using fMRI and multivoxel pattern analysis in macaques. Robust orientation information was detected in the inferotemporal (IT) cortex, distributed specifically within functional 'hubs' characterized by multivariate responsiveness to multiple object categories (faces, bodies, objects) and enhanced connectivity with upstream areas (V1–V4). This phenomenon was replicated in deep convolutional neural network models, where hub-like units supported hierarchical processing of complex inputs. These results imply that orientation information in high-level visual areas arises as a computational byproduct of functional diversity and network connectivity rather than redundant feature encoding, facilitating integrative visual processing.

### 2025 — Task-Irrelevant Phase but not Contrast Variability Unlocks Generalization in Visual Perceptual Learning
*Journal of Cognitive Enhancement*
Authors: Beyza Akkoyunlu, Caspar M. Schwiedrzik

While visual perceptual learning (VPL) often results in highly specific performance gains restricted to trained retinal locations, this study investigated whether task-irrelevant stimulus variability can induce generalization. Subjects practiced an orientation discrimination task where trial-by-trial variability was introduced in either spatial phase or contrast. The results demonstrated that randomizing spatial phase led to complete spatial generalization of learning to untrained locations, whereas contrast randomization did not. This dissociation suggests that the neural substrates recruited for plasticity are contingent upon the nature of the task-irrelevant noise; variability in features processed with invariance in higher-order areas allows the visual system to utilize representations that facilitate broader transfer and overcome traditional learning specificity.

### 2024 — Predictions enable top-down pattern separation in the macaque face-processing hierarchy
*Nature Communications*
Authors: Tarana Nigam, Caspar M. Schwiedrzik

Using fMRI in macaques, this research examined how top-down predictive signals, derived from statistical learning of face sequences, modulate neural representational geometries in the ventral visual stream. The authors found that contextual predictions significantly enhance pattern separability and increase the dimensionality of neural activity patterns in early stages of the face-processing hierarchy (ML/MF). These lower-level areas dynamically inherited higher-order, invariant tuning properties from the top-level area (AM), effectively transforming their feedforward representations. These findings provide evidence that top-down predictive context reconfigures representational spaces throughout cortical hierarchies to optimize the distinction of facial identities and facilitate efficient recognition.

### 2024 — Visual perceptual learning of feature conjunctions leverages non-linear mixed selectivity
*npj Science of Learning*
Authors: Behnam Karami, Caspar M. Schwiedrzik

Learning to identify visual objects often necessitates the integration of multiple features. This study employed psychophysical experiments and neural modeling to investigate how the visual system achieves orientation-color conjunction learning. The results show that while conjunction learning can occur via linear combinations of pure feature information, the most efficient and rapid learning involves non-linear mixed selectivity. Furthermore, subjects who utilized mixed-selectivity representations during training demonstrated superior performance on an untrained 'exclusive or' (XOR) task months later. These results suggest that the primate brain utilizes high-dimensional, non-linear representational spaces to support the flexible acquisition and generalization of complex multi-feature visual concepts.
