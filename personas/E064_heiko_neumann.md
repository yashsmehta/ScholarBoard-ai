# Heiko Neumann

*Computational vision and neural modeling* — Ulm University, Institute of Neural Information Processing, Vision and Perception Science Group.

## Background

Heiko Neumann is a Senior Professor at the Institute of Neural Information Processing at Ulm University and leads the Vision and Perception Science group. Neumann's research uses neural modeling to investigate the computational principles of visual information processing in biological systems and artificial architectures. They study the dynamics of motion perception, object recognition, and visual attention, focusing on how hierarchical neural circuits integrate contextual information for task-relevant behavior. Neumann's theoretical findings are applied to domains including autonomous robotics, medical image analysis, and human-computer interaction.

## Papers

### 2025 — A model of thalamo-cortical interaction for incremental binding in mental contour-tracing
*PLOS Computational Biology*
Authors: Daniel Schmid, Heiko Neumann

This study introduces a mesoscopic neural dynamical architecture that elucidates the mechanisms of object-based attention and incremental binding. The model posits that perceptual grouping occurs via an attentional spreading process, where neurons representing bound object elements are 'tagged' by sustained activity enhancement. Mechanistically, the model integrates feedforward feature extraction with recurrent thalamo-cortical loops, proposed to involve pyramidal cell integration hubs. The architecture suggests that apical dendritic signals carrying contextual grouping information are gated by higher-order thalamic nuclei (e.g., the pulvinar) based on task-relevance, providing a biologically plausible implementation of the 'growth-cone' hypothesis for mental contour grouping. Model performance was validated against neurophysiological data on the speed and scale-invariance of contour labeling.

### 2025 — Application of Scene Perception and Object Detection Technology Based on Computer Vision in Autonomous Driving for Public Transportation Route Optimization
*Applied and Computational Engineering*
Authors: Peiqi Chen, Daniel Schmid, Christian Jarvers, Heiko Neumann

This paper explores the integration of computer vision-based scene perception and Geographic Information System (GIS) data for dynamic route optimization in autonomous public transit systems. The proposed framework employs deep learning architectures for real-time object detection and semantic segmentation to perceive environment variations, such as traffic congestion and physical obstructions. By blending visual scene understanding with GIS-based topographic information, the system dynamically recalculates optimal transit paths. Simulation results in virtual city environments indicate significant reductions in travel latency and energy consumption compared to traditional fixed-route approaches, highlighting the potential for computer vision to improve the scalability and safety of intelligent urban infrastructure.

### 2024 — Teaching deep networks to see shape: Lessons from a simplified visual world
*PLOS Computational Biology*
Authors: Christian Jarvers, Heiko Neumann

This research investigates the discrepancy between the strong shape-dependency of primate vision and the pervasive texture/color bias in deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs). Using synthetic datasets with controlled feature distributions (e.g., rectangles, 'L' and 'T' shapes), the authors demonstrate that while standard architectures can learn shape features in isolation, they systematically default to simpler surface-level cues when available. Through neural tangent kernel (NTK) analysis, the study shows that this bias is driven by the dynamics of mini-batch gradient descent, where weight updates for diverse, sparse shape features are effectively suppressed by consistent, dense updates from low-level features. The findings suggest that reaching human-level shape robustness in artificial vision requires novel learning algorithms characterized by sparser and more local weight adaptations.

### 2024 — Cycle-Correspondence Loss: Learning Dense View-Invariant Visual Features from Unlabeled and Unordered RGB Images
*IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*
Authors: David B. Adrian, Andras Gabor Kupcsik, Markus Spies, Heiko Neumann

The authors propose Cycle-Correspondence Loss (CCL), a self-supervised training objective for learning view-invariant dense visual descriptors from uncalibrated and unordered sets of RGB images. CCL leverages the principle of cycle-consistency to autonomously detect valid pixel-level correspondences between disparate viewpoints by performing confidence-weighted bidirectional predictions. Unlike previous methods requiring registered RGB-D sequences or multi-view consistency labels, CCL enables robust keypoint learning from a single moving camera. Evaluation on keypoint tracking and robotic manipulation tasks demonstrates that CCL outperforms existing self-supervised RGB baselines and approaches the accuracy of fully supervised methods in dense object-centric representation learning.

### 2024 — Multimodal vision-based human action recognition using deep learning: a review
*Artificial Intelligence Review*
Authors: Fatemeh Shafizadegan, Ahmad Reza Naghsh-Nilchi, Elham Shabaninia, Heiko Neumann

This comprehensive review categorizes and analyzes recent advancements in multimodal human action recognition (HAR) using deep learning. The authors provide a taxonomy of fusion strategies across four levels: data-level, feature-level, decision-level, and hybrid fusion. The study evaluates how the integration of complementary modalities—such as RGB, depth, skeleton, and infrared data—enhances recognition accuracy and robustness compared to single-modality systems. Furthermore, the review explores the application of self-attention mechanisms and transformer-based architectures for distilling spatio-temporal features across heterogeneous data streams. It concludes by identifying key challenges in the field, including cross-dataset generalization and the computational complexity of real-time multi-stream inference.
