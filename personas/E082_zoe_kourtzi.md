# Zoe Kourtzi

*computational cognitive neuroscience* — University of Cambridge, Department of Psychology, Adaptive Brain Lab.

## Background

Zoe Kourtzi is a Professor of Experimental Psychology and Computational Cognitive Neuroscience at the University of Cambridge and the head of the Adaptive Brain Lab. Kourtzi’s research integrates multimodal neuroimaging, including 7T fMRI, EEG, and MRS, with machine learning to investigate the neural mechanisms of brain plasticity and lifelong learning. They examine how humans translate sensory experiences into adaptive decisions and develop predictive AI-guided models for the early diagnosis of neurodegenerative diseases and mental health disorders. This work connects systems neuroscience with translational neuroinformatics in the study of vision science and clinical diagnostics.

## Papers

### 2025 — AI-guided patient stratification improves outcomes and efficiency in the AMARANTH Alzheimer's Disease clinical trial
*Nature Communications*
Authors: Delshad Vaghari, Gayathri Mohankumar, Keith Tan, Andrew Lowe, Christopher Shering, Peter Tino, Zoe Kourtzi

This study demonstrates that an AI-guided predictive prognostic model (PPM) can enhance patient stratification in Alzheimer's disease (AD) clinical trials by mitigating the impact of patient heterogeneity. The researchers applied the PPM, which utilizes baseline biological markers (including β-amyloid, APOE ε4 status, and medial temporal lobe grey matter density), to re-evaluate the AMARANTH trial of lanabecestat, a BACE1 inhibitor previously deemed futile. The model successfully stratified participants into slow- and rapid-progression subgroups. While the original trial showed no significant effect in the total population, the AI-guided analysis revealed that lanabecestat significantly slowed cognitive decline by 46% in the subgroup of individuals at the earliest stages of cognitive impairment. These findings highlight the potential for AI-driven precision medicine to improve clinical trial efficiency and rescue efficacious treatments through refined patient selection.

### 2025 — Neurogenetic phenotypes of learning-dependent plasticity for improved perceptual decisions
*Communications Biology*
Authors: Liz Yuanxi Lee, Joseph J Ziminski, Polytimi Frangou, Vasilis M Karlaftis, Yezhou Wang, Boris Bernhardt, Varun Warrier, Richard A I Bethlehem, Zoe Kourtzi

This research investigates the link between brain-wide gene expression and macroscopic neuroimaging phenotypes of learning-dependent plasticity in the adult human brain. Combining functional connectivity (FC) analysis and quantitative multi-parameter mapping (MPM) of cortical microstructure, the authors demonstrate that training on a perceptual decision task (discriminating Glass patterns in noise) induces specific spatial variations in cortical gradients. These plasticity markers are significantly linked to the expression of genes associated with neuronal signaling and synaptic organization within visual and fronto-parietal networks. Furthermore, the study shows that anodal transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) targeted at the visual cortex during training enhances learning and modulates functional connections without altering microstructural organization. These results reveal neurogenetic phenotypes of plasticity, providing a cross-scale framework that bridges molecular mechanisms and macroscopic brain dynamics supporting adaptive behavior.

### 2024 — Recurrent inhibition refines mental templates to optimize perceptual decisions
*Science Advances*
Authors: Ke Jia, Mengxin Wang, Cecilia Steinwurzel, Joseph J. Ziminski, Yinghua Xi, Uzay Emir, Zoe Kourtzi

The authors investigate the neural mechanisms underlying the formation of mental templates for perceptual decision-making using ultrahigh-field (7-T) functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS). Focusing on orientation discrimination, the study demonstrates that perceptual training induces recurrent inhibition within the superficial layers of the visual cortex. Specifically, the findings reveal an increase in γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA)-mediated inhibition and enhanced discriminability of orientation representations in superficial layers (rather than middle or deep layers). Computational modeling suggests that these fine-scale functional and neurochemical interactions refine task-relevant internal templates, thereby optimizing the brain's capacity to translate sensory inputs into adaptive perceptual decisions. This work provides a mechanistic account of how laminar-specific cortical plasticity supports cognitive flexibility through experience.

### 2024 — Robust and interpretable AI-guided marker for early dementia prediction in real-world clinical settings
*EClinicalMedicine*
Authors: Liz Yuanxi Lee, Delshad Vaghari, Michael C Burkhart, Peter Tino, Marcella Montagnese, Zhuoyu Li, Katharina Zühlsdorff, Joseph Giorgio, Guy Williams, Eddie Chong, Christopher Chen, Benjamin R Underwood, Timothy Rittman, Zoe Kourtzi

This paper presents the development and validation of a robust, interpretable AI-guided marker for early dementia prediction, designed for real-world clinical applicability. The Predictive Prognostic Model (PPM) integrates routinely-collected, non-invasive data, specifically structural MRI and cognitive assessments. Validation across multiple international cohorts—including the ADNI research database and real-world memory clinic data from the UK and Singapore—demonstrated that the PPM predicts progression from mild cognitive impairment (MCI) to Alzheimer's disease (AD) with significantly higher sensitivity than current clinical standards. The derived individualized prognostic index achieved a hazard ratio of 3.42 compared to standard grey matter atrophy and cognitive scores. The model correctly identified 82% of individuals who progressed to AD and 81% of those who remained stable, offering a scalable tool for patient stratification and early intervention.

### 2024 — Unsupervised multimodal modeling of cognitive and brain health trajectories for early dementia prediction
*Scientific Reports*
Authors: Michael C Burkhart, Liz Y Lee, Delshad Vaghari, An Qi Toh, Eddie Chong, Christopher Chen, Peter Tiňo, Zoe Kourtzi

The study introduces an unsupervised multimodal trajectory modeling (MTM) approach to address the challenge of early dementia prediction in the absence of labeled clinical diagnoses. Utilizing a mixture of state-space models, the MTM framework learns to capture longitudinal changes in cognitive performance and invasive biological markers (e.g., β-amyloid levels and grey matter density). The model successfully stratifies individuals into clinically meaningful clusters based on their trajectories of cognitive and brain health, demonstrating superior reliability compared to models trained on baseline data alone. Crucially, the MTM generalizes from research cohorts to heterogeneous real-world patient data with missing observations. By deriving an individualized cognitive health index, the approach enables more precise prediction of Alzheimer's disease progression than conventional MRI or cognitive metrics, providing a cost-effective, data-driven solution for clinical prognosis.
