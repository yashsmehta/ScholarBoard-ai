# Stanislas Dehaene

*Cognitive neuroscience of consciousness and literacy* — Collège de France, Experimental Cognitive Psychology, Cognitive Neuroimaging Unit (Unicog).

## Background

Stanislas Dehaene investigates the neural architecture underlying high-level human functions, particularly reading, mathematics, and consciousness. Dehaene characterized the 'visual word form area' in the ventral visual stream and identified the 'number sense' in the intraparietal sulcus, illustrating how education repurposes evolutionarily older cortical circuits. Using functional neuroimaging (fMRI, M/EEG), experimental psychology, and computational modeling, their research explores the transition from subliminal to conscious processing. This work currently focuses on the Global Neuronal Workspace Theory and the neural codes governing hierarchical symbolic representations in the human brain.

## Papers

### 2025 — From retinotopic to ordinal coding: Dissecting the cortical stages of visual word recognition
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*
Authors: Aakash Agrawal, Stanislas Dehaene

This study utilizes 7-Tesla functional MRI and magnetoencephalography (MEG) to delineate the cortical transformation of visual word representations along the human ventral stream. The research identifies a hierarchy where early visual areas (V1-V4) primarily encode retinotopic letter positions, whereas higher-order regions, specifically the visual word form area (VWFA), transition to an invariant ordinal letter-position code. MEG time-course analysis reveals that retinotopic signals for word and letter position manifest between 60 and 200 ms, followed by the emergence of abstract ordinal representations in the 220–450 ms window. These findings elucidate the spatiotemporal dynamics by which the brain converts sensory visual input into structured prelexical orthographic codes.

### 2025 — Rapid emergence of a maths gender gap in first grade
*Nature*
Authors: P. Martinot, B. Colnet, T. Breda, J. Sultan, L. Touitou, P. Huguet, E. Spelke, G. Dehaene-Lambertz, P. Bressoux, S. Dehaene

A massive 4-year longitudinal study involving 2,653,082 French schoolchildren examined the emergence of gender disparities in mathematics. While boys and girls demonstrated nearly identical performance levels at the start of primary school, a significant gender gap favoring boys appeared within four months of schooling and expanded to an effect size of roughly 0.20 after one year. This trend was robust across different socioeconomic levels and school types. By leveraging near-orthogonal variations in age and schooling, the authors conclude that the gap is driven by the schooling environment rather than chronological age, highlighting the first year of primary education as a critical period for the entrenchment of sociocultural math stereotypes.

### 2025 — A geometric shape regularity effect in the human brain
*eLife*
Authors: Mathias Sablé-Meyer, Lucas Benjamin, Cassandra Potier Watkins, Chenxi He, Maxence Pajot, Théo Morfoisse, Fosca Al Roumi, Stanislas Dehaene

This research combines fMRI and MEG in adults and six-year-olds to investigate the neural correlates of human geometric perception. The study identifies two distinct brain systems: an evolutionarily ancient occipitotemporal network for visual object recognition, well-modeled by feedforward convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and a specifically human dorso-frontal system that encodes discrete geometric regularities such as symmetry and parallelism. These symbolic representations appear approximately 150 ms post-stimulus and are better predicted by a 'Language-of-Thought' model than by visual hierarchies. Geometric regularity was found to modulate activation in mathematical processing regions, including the intraparietal sulcus and inferior temporal cortex, suggesting that abstract geometry engages a symbolic mode of perception.

### 2025 — Mapping and modeling the semantic space of math concepts
*Cognition*
Authors: Samuel Debray, Stanislas Dehaene

The authors map the global semantic structure of mathematical concepts by analyzing behavioral similarity judgments for the 1,000 most frequent math terms in French and English. Using distributed semantic embeddings (GloVe) and behavioral data, the study demonstrates that approximately 50% of human judgment variance is explained by co-occurrence statistics. The analysis reveals that mathematical expertise, quantified by education level, modulates the familiarity and hierarchical entanglement of concepts. The resulting database provides a comprehensive map of mathematical space, incorporating lexical frequency, age of acquisition, and semantic connectivity to delineate the cognitive architecture of mathematical knowledge.

### 2024 — Cracking the neural code for word recognition in convolutional neural networks
*PLOS Computational Biology*
Authors: Aakash Agrawal, Stanislas Dehaene

This computational study utilizes convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to investigate how the visual system is 'recycled' for reading. By retraining ImageNet-initialized networks on word recognition, the authors analyze the emergence of reading-specific units. The findings show a hierarchical transition from retinotopic feature detection to ordinal position coding, achieved through specialized 'space bigram' units that detect letter identities relative to word boundaries (blank spaces). The proposed architecture provides a mechanistic account for the emergence of invariant word recognition in the visual word form area (VWFA) and successfully predicts human behavioral patterns and neurophysiological data.
