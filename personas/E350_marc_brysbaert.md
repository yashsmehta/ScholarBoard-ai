# Marc Brysbaert

*visual word recognition and reading* — Ghent University, Department of Experimental Psychology, Center for Reading Research.

## Background

Marc Brysbaert is a Professor of Psychology at Ghent University whose research centers on the cognitive and neural mechanisms of visual word recognition and reading. Brysbaert developed the SUBTLEX word frequency norms and examines hemispheric lateralization in language processing, including the split-fovea theory. Their research utilizes a combination of behavioral megastudies, eye-tracking, and neuroimaging to investigate how lexical variables influence language acquisition and bilingualism. Brysbaert currently focuses on integrating big data and open science methodologies to improve the reliability and reproducibility of psycholinguistic benchmarks.

## Papers

### 2026 — Updating the German Psycholinguistic Word Toolbox with AI-Generated Estimates of Concreteness, Valence, Arousal, Age of Acquisition, and Familiarity
*Journal of Cognition*
Authors: Javier Conde, Gonzalo Martínez, María Grandury, Carlos Arriaga, Juan Haro, Sascha Schroeder, Florian Hintz, Pedro Reviriego, Marc Brysbaert

The study generated psycholinguistic estimates for approximately 185,000 German words using the GPT-4o-mini large language model. Five critical word features—concreteness, valence, arousal, age of acquisition (AoA), and familiarity—were estimated and validated against existing human-rated behavioral norms and datasets. Statistical validation through Spearman correlations demonstrated that AI-generated familiarity estimates significantly outperformed traditional corpus-based word frequency measures in predicting human performance on lexical decision tasks and vocabulary benchmarks. The resulting dataset serves as a comprehensive resource for experimental control in German linguistic and cognitive research.

### 2025 — Simulating lexical decision times with large language models to supplement megastudies and crowdsourcing
*Behavior Research Methods*
Authors: Gonzalo Martínez, Javier Conde, Pedro Reviriego, Marc Brysbaert

This research investigates the utility of fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) to predict human reaction times (RTs) in visual word recognition tasks. By fine-tuning GPT-4o-mini on a seed set of 3,000 words from an existing megastudy, the authors demonstrated that the model could accurately generate RT estimates for the remaining lexical items in the corpus, achieving high correlations with empirical human data. The paper discusses specific applications for this 'virtual participant' methodology, including filling gaps in sparse megastudy data, verifying experimental designs through simulation, and optimizing stimulus selection for subsequent human behavioral experiments.

### 2025 — Applying Mixed-Effects Models in Research on Second Language Acquisition: A Tutorial for Beginners
*Languages*
Authors: Marc Brysbaert

This methodological tutorial addresses the limitations of traditional ANOVA-based analyses (F1 and F2) in second language acquisition (SLA) research, advocating for the adoption of linear mixed-effects (LME) models. The guide provides a step-by-step framework for implementing LME in the user-friendly jamovi software using the GAMLj package. It emphasizes the handling of crossed random effects for both participants and stimuli, the advantages of modeling unbalanced data without loss of power, and the specific considerations for effect size calculation and power analysis in L2 learning contexts.

### 2024 — Establishing vocabulary tests as a benchmark for evaluating large language models
*PLOS ONE*
Authors: Gonzalo Martínez, Jessie S. Barrot, Javier Conde, Elena Merino-Gómez, Beatriz Bermúdez-Margaretto, José Alberto Hernández, Pedro Reviriego, Marc Brysbaert

This study proposes the use of human vocabulary assessments as a rigorous benchmark for evaluating the linguistic competence and lexical coverage of large language models (LLMs). The performance of seven state-of-the-art models, including GPT-4 and Llama 2, was assessed using two standardized vocabulary test formats across English and Spanish. The findings revealed that while models perform exceptionally well on common lexical items, they exhibit significant and unpredictable gaps in low-frequency and specialized vocabulary knowledge. The authors advocate for the inclusion of these linguistic benchmarks to complement existing domain-specific and task-oriented evaluation metrics.

### 2024 — Ratings of valence, arousal, happiness, anger, fear, sadness, disgust, and surprise for 24,000 Dutch words
*Behavior Research Methods*
Authors: Laura J. Speed, Marc Brysbaert

To facilitate large-scale research on the interplay between emotion and language, this study compiled a comprehensive database of affective norms for 24,000 Dutch words. Using a crowdsourcing approach, word-level ratings were collected for the primary affective dimensions of valence and arousal, alongside six discrete emotion categories (happiness, anger, fear, sadness, disgust, and surprise). Regression analyses on lexical decision times confirmed that emotional variables significantly predict word processing speed, with a facilitator effect for positive words. The dataset includes additional psycholinguistic controls such as word frequency, length, and concreteness to support multi-variable modeling of lexical processing.
